## VERSION A: Casual (Existing Relationships)

**Use for:** Furlows, Lyons, Westbrooks, McLeods, Britans -- anyone you already have a relationship with.

**Deploy as:** A natural paragraph inside an existing email (trip update, validation email, payment follow-up). Not a standalone message. Not a subject line. Just John mentioning something.

---

**Example placement -- inside any normal client email, after the main content:**

> One thing I wanted to mention -- I use some smart tools on my end to keep track of your preferences, trip details, and our conversations so I can do a better job for you. Things like your dining preferences, cabin likes, excursion history, that kind of thing. What I don't track is your financial information beyond what's on the booking, and I never share any of it with anyone. Period. If you'd ever rather I not keep notes like that, just let me know and I'll stop -- no questions, no hassle. I just want to make sure you know how I'm working to give you better service every trip.

---

**EXEC Notes on Version A:**
- No greeting or sign-off included -- this lives inside an existing email that already has those
- Tone matches John's "worth knowing" pattern -- presenting information, not asking permission
- The word "smart tools" is deliberate. Not "AI," not "artificial intelligence," not "machine learning." Clients don't need the technical layer. They need the truth: tools that remember
- "Period." is a John move -- short, certain, full stop
- Opt-out is one sentence, buried naturally, not a button or a form

### When to Send to Existing Clients

**Before auto-CRM goes live.** Roll it out naturally over the next 2-3 weeks:

1. **Furlows** -- Include Version A in the next payment/validation email (Apr 1 payment approaching, natural touchpoint)
2. **Westbrooks** -- Include in next Silver Nova update
3. **McLeods / McGlassons** -- Include in next Mediterranean prep email
4. **Lyons** -- Include when following up on Athens dinner reservation or transfers
5. **Britans** -- Next dining/flight follow-up
6. **Kuklinskis, Nichols, Ely/Darrow** -- Next scheduled touchpoint

**Do not send a blast.** Each client gets it embedded in a real conversation at a natural moment. That's the whole point.

### How to Handle "No Thanks"

If a client opts out of preference tracking:

1. **Acknowledge immediately.** "Done. I've cleared your notes. Appreciate you telling me."
2. **Flag in dossier.** Add to client profile: `PRIVACY: OPT-OUT -- no preference tracking. Manual notes only.`
3. **Dani behavior.** Dani does not reference stored preferences for this client. Every interaction starts fresh.
4. **No follow-up.** Do not ask why. Do not try to change their mind. Do not mention it again.
5. **Service continues.** They still get the same quality. It just takes more back-and-forth to get there.

**The principle:** An opt-out is not a rejection. It's a preference -- and we respect preferences. That's the whole business.

## PADRE'S NOTE

Yoda -- I sat with this one. Naia and I went back and forth on whether to use the word "AI" anywhere. We landed on no. Here's why.

"AI" means different things to different people right now. To some it's helpful, to others it's unsettling. "Smart tools" and "intelligent tools" are honest without triggering a reaction that has nothing to do with you or how you actually use them. You're not hiding anything -- the door is open if anyone asks. But the covenant should build trust, not start a conversation about technology.

The Nancy test: Would Nancy Lyons read this and feel comfortable? I believe she would. It sounds like you talking. It tells her what you do and why. It tells her what you don't do. And it gives her a simple way to say no. That's respect.

One more thing. The opt-out language is deliberately light -- "just let me know" / "just say the word." No checkboxes, no forms, no legalese. Because the moment you make it feel like a legal process, you've told the client this is about protecting you, not serving them. And that's not who you are.
