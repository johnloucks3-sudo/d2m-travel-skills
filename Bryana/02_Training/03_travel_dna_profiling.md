# Travel DNA Profiling — Reading Your Client
## Dreams2Memories Travel, LLC | Bryana Roelke Training Module 2.6

---

## What This Is

Every traveler has a "Travel DNA" — a personality type that drives what they actually want from a trip, even when they can't articulate it themselves. Your job as an advisor is to identify that DNA early, because it changes everything: which products you recommend, how you write to them, what you lead with, what you avoid.

D2M uses an 8-category image-tap onboarding tool for new clients (categories: Excursions, Flight, Hotel, Cabin, Sea Day, Shore, Dining, Evening). Clients pick the images they're drawn to — and the pattern tells us who they are.

You don't always have the form. But you can read the same signals through conversation.

---

## The 8 Travel DNA Archetypes

### 1. The Romantic Escapist
**Who they are:** Couples (or solo travelers who travel as if they're on a date with life). They want to feel something. The trip is about *presence* — candles, quiet, slow mornings, the view from the balcony.

**What they say:**
- "We just want to get away."
- "Somewhere peaceful."
- "We don't need to pack in activities."
- "It's our anniversary / honeymoon / we need this."

**What they buy:** Veranda cabins. Specialty dining. Spa credits. Silversea, Regent, Seabourn. Champagne on embarkation. Quiet ports over busy ones.

**What NOT to lead with:** Group excursions. Itinerary complexity. Price comparisons.

---

### 2. The Adventure Purist
**Who they are:** Physical. Story-driven. They want the photograph that stops dinner parties. They'll hike, kayak, snorkel, zip-line — and they want to tell people about it.

**What they say:**
- "We want to be active."
- "We've done the Caribbean — we want something different."
- "What's the most unique destination?"
- "We don't want to just sit on a ship."

**What they buy:** Expedition lines (Lindblad, Ponant, Aurora). Shore excursions heavy on activity. Galapagos. Antarctica. Alaska. Smaller ships that go places big ships can't.

**What NOT to lead with:** Sea day programs. Formal dining. Mainstream itineraries.

---

### 3. The Cultural Connoisseur
**Who they are:** Intellectual. Unhurried. They read before they travel. They want to understand a place, not just photograph it. They appreciate craftsmanship — in food, in architecture, in local tradition.

**What they say:**
- "We want to really experience the culture."
- "We're interested in history."
- "We love food — real food, not tourist food."
- "We've read a lot about [destination]."

**What they buy:** River cruises (AmaWaterways, Viking). Cultural immersion shore excursions. Chef's table dinners. Enrichment lecture programs. Mediterranean, Japan, India itineraries.

**What NOT to lead with:** Adventure or physical activities. Entertainment-heavy itineraries. Mass-market lines.

---

### 4. The Luxury Minimalist
**Who they are:** High-earning, taste-conscious, low-drama. They want perfection delivered quietly. They don't want to think about logistics — they want things to just *work*. They judge by what's absent (crowds, noise, waiting) as much as what's present.

**What they say:**
- "We just want it handled."
- "We don't want to deal with crowds."
- "We've traveled a lot — we know what we like."
- "I don't need a lot of extras, just quality."

**What they buy:** Suite-class or small-ship luxury. Silversea, Seabourn. Direct flights, no layovers. Private transfers. Pre-reserved everything.

**What NOT to lead with:** Deals, promotions, or "value" framing. Complicated multi-stop itineraries unless they ask.

---

### 5. The Social Architect
**Who they are:** Energetic, outward-facing. They want to maximize the experience — shows, pool deck, group dinners, meeting people. They often travel with friends or family. The trip is a shared memory-making event.

**What they say:**
- "We're going with a group of friends / family."
- "We want to have fun."
- "We like a lively atmosphere."
- "What's the nightlife like?"

**What they buy:** Premium lines with strong entertainment programs. Viking, Celebrity, Oceania on the higher end. Group excursions. Specialty restaurants with a social atmosphere.

**What NOT to lead with:** Quiet, intimate, or adult-only experiences unless the group is an older couple pairing.

---

### 6. The Contemplative Voyager
**Who they are:** Quiet. Internal. They travel to think, to reset, to find perspective. They're often the most loyal clients because when you give them the right experience, it goes deep. They're not passive — they're observational.

**What they say:**
- "I just need to decompress."
- "I love sea days."
- "I don't need to do a lot — I like to wander."
- "I want to read and just... be somewhere beautiful."

**What they buy:** Longer voyages. Crossing itineraries. Lines with excellent library programs and lecture series (Silversea Expedition, Cunard). Itineraries with off-the-beaten-path ports.

**What NOT to lead with:** Packed excursion schedules. High-energy entertainment. Short cruises.

---

### 7. The Aspirational First-Timer
**Who they are:** Wide-eyed, excited, sometimes anxious. This is their first real luxury experience — they want to do it *right* but they're not sure what right looks like. They're highly coachable. They trust you to guide them.

**What they say:**
- "We've never done anything like this before."
- "We don't know where to start."
- "What would you recommend?"
- "We saved up for this."

**What they buy:** Whatever you guide them toward. Start them on the right line for their personality — this is your most important placement decision. A wrong first experience = never comes back. Right first experience = client for life + 3 referrals.

**What NOT to do:** Oversell. Underdeliver. Put them on the wrong line for who they are.

---

### 8. The Seasoned Sophisticate
**Who they are:** Comparison-ready. They've done it before — possibly many times. They know what the Silversea butler service is *supposed* to look like and they'll tell you if it doesn't. They're valuable clients and exacting ones.

**What they say:**
- "We've sailed Regent before."
- "Last time, the [specific thing] wasn't as good as..."
- "What's new that we haven't done?"
- "We want something different."

**What they buy:** Whatever has genuinely moved the needle since their last trip. Expedition lines they haven't tried. New itineraries on lines they love. Upgrades they haven't experienced.

**What NOT to do:** Pitch them something they've done. Or something you can't back up with specifics. They will know.

## Reading the Signals in Conversation

You don't always get image-tap data. Here's how to profile in real time:

| If they say... | Likely archetype |
|---|---|
| "Just the two of us" + anniversary/honeymoon context | Romantic Escapist |
| "Active," "unique," "bucket list" | Adventure Purist |
| "Culture," "history," "food scene," "local experience" | Cultural Connoisseur |
| "Just handled," "no hassle," "we've traveled a lot" | Luxury Minimalist |
| "Group," "fun," "lively," bringing friends | Social Architect |
| "Sea days," "decompress," "wander" | Contemplative Voyager |
| "Never done this before," "don't know where to start" | Aspirational First-Timer |
| "We've done Regent," "last time on Silversea" | Seasoned Sophisticate |

## Couple Dynamics (Critical Skill)

Most bookings are two people. They are often *different archetypes* — and one drives the decision.

**Common pairings and how to handle them:**

**Romantic Escapist + Adventure Purist:** One wants romance, one wants stories. Serve both: cultural excursions during the day (satisfies the purist), elegant evenings (satisfies the escapist). The right ship has both.

**Cultural Connoisseur + Social Architect:** One wants to go deep, one wants to have fun. Look for lines with strong enrichment programs AND good social atmosphere. Viking Ocean does this well.

**Luxury Minimalist + Aspirational First-Timer:** One knows exactly what they want, one is overwhelmed. Lead with the minimalist's priorities — the first-timer will follow the confident partner.

**How to find the decision-driver:** Ask "what matters most to each of you?" Watch who answers first and with more specificity. That person is your client relationship anchor.

---

## The Practical Use

Once you have an archetype:

1. **Lead product recommendations from it.** Don't show a Contemplative Voyager a 7-night Caribbean party cruise. Don't show an Adventure Purist a mainstream Mediterranean itinerary without adventure excursions.

2. **Match your tone.** Romantic Escapists get warm, evocative language. Luxury Minimalists get clean and confident. Aspirational First-Timers get warmth and guidance. Seasoned Sophisticates get specificity and respect for what they already know.

3. **Anticipate the objection.** A Luxury Minimalist will push back on anything complicated. A Seasoned Sophisticate will question whether it's actually different. Build your answer to their archetype's objection before they raise it.

4. **Use it to set up Navarro profiles.** When you hand off a client to D2M systems, the Travel DNA profile is your first contribution. It shapes everything downstream — itinerary, dining reservations, excursion recommendations, email copy.
