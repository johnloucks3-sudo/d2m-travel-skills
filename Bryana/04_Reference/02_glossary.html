Industry Glossary

Common terms every advisor should know

| Term | What It Means | |---|---| | All-inclusive | A cruise fare that includes gratuities, beverages, specialty dining, and sometimes shore excursions and air. Standard in luxury (Silversea, Regent, Seabourn). | | Azipod | A propulsion system that can rotate 360 degrees, giving ships exceptional maneuverability in ports. | | Base fare | The cruise price before taxes, fees, port charges, and any promotions are applied. | | B2B (Back-to-Back) | Two consecutive sailings on the same ship, often with different itineraries. Clients stay in the same cabin. | | Brochure fare | The published retail price before any promotions, discounts, or loyalty benefits are applied. Almost nobody pays this. | | Category | The cabin class level (e.g., Vista Suite, Deluxe Veranda, Penthouse). Each line uses different naming. | | CLIA | Cruise Lines International Association. The industry trade organization. Membership gives access to training, certifications, and industry resources. | | Commissionable | The portion of a fare on which the travel advisor earns commission. Usually the base fare minus taxes/fees. | | Concierge | A dedicated service desk on board that handles dining reservations, excursion bookings, and special requests. In luxury lines, every suite gets concierge access. | | Destination Services | The onboard team that manages shore excursions, port information, and destination-related questions. | | Deviation | Flying in early or staying late relative to a cruise, often managed by the cruise line's air department. May incur a fee. | | Dossier | A structured client file containing booking details, preferences, dietary needs, special requests, payment status, and correspondence history. Each D2M client has one. | | Embarkation | The process of boarding the ship at the start of the cruise. | | FPD (Final Payment Date) | The date by which the full balance must be paid. Usually 90-120 days before sailing for luxury lines. The most important deadline in client management. | | Gratuities | Tips for onboard staff — cabin stewards, dining staff, bartenders. In luxury lines, gratuities are included in the fare. | | Gross Tonnage (GT) | A measure of a ship's internal volume, not weight. Used to compare ship sizes. Larger GT usually means more amenities. | | Group Booking | A block of cabins held under a single booking with negotiated benefits (reduced deposit, OBC, or special pricing). Requires a minimum number of cabins. | | Guarantee | A booking where the cruise line assigns the specific cabin later. You are guaranteed at minimum the category you booked or higher. Lower price, less control. | | Included | What is included in the fare. In luxury: drinks, gratuities, specialty dining, often excursions and flights. In premium: varies. | | Itinerary | The full schedule of ports, sea days, and onboard events for a specific sailing. | | Loyalty Program | Each line has one (e.g., Silversea's Venetian Society, Regent's Seven Seas Society). Benefits include discounts, cabin upgrades, OBC, and priority boarding. | | Luxury Line | Silversea, Regent, Seabourn, Explora, Ponant. Smaller ships, higher space ratios, more inclusions, higher per-day pricing. | | MBO (Onboard Booking / Future Cruise) | A booking made while currently on a cruise. Usually comes with reduced deposit and OBC. The highest-margin booking type. | | Net Rate | The wholesale price the travel agency pays before commission is applied. | | Non-Commissionable Fees (NCF) | Port taxes, fees, and other charges on which no commission is paid. | | OBC (Onboard Credit) | A credit applied to the client's onboard account for spending on excursions, dining, spa, shops, or gratuities. Can be from the cruise line, travel advisor, or promotions. | | Occupancy | The number of passengers carried divided by total berths. Double occupancy = 2 per cabin. Single occupancy = 1 per cabin (often with a supplement). | | Premium Line | Viking, Oceania, Azamara, Holland America, Celebrity. Larger ships, more itineraries, fewer inclusions, lower per-day pricing. | | Port Charges | Fees the cruise line pays to each port of call. Included in the fare. Not commissionable. | | Pre-/Post-Cruise | Hotel stays and transfers before or after the cruise. Often packaged by the cruise line. | | Promotion | A limited-time offer: reduced deposits, OBC, beverage packages, or fare discounts. | | Repositioning (Repo) | A one-way sailing that moves a ship between seasonal regions (e.g., Alaska to Hawaii in fall). Often longer, with unusual port combinations and deep discounts. | | River Cruise | Inland waterway cruising on smaller ships (100-200 passengers). Destinations: European rivers, Mekong, Amazon, Nile, Mississippi. All-inclusive is standard. | | Sea Day | A full day at sea with no port call. Ships plan enrichment activities (lectures, cooking demos, wine tastings). | | Shore Excursion | A guided tour or activity at a port of call. Booked through the ship or independently. Ship bookings are more expensive but guaranteed to wait if the tour runs late. | | Single Supplement | An additional charge for a solo traveler occupying a cabin designed for two. Typically 50-100% of the per-person fare. | | Space Ratio | Gross tonnage divided by passenger capacity. Higher ratio = more space per passenger = less crowded. Luxury ships typically have ratios above 50. | | Specialty Dining | Alternative restaurants on board beyond the main dining room. May be included (luxury) or at extra charge (premium). | | Stateroom | Industry term for a cruise cabin. Names vary by line: Suite, Veranda, Oceanview, Inside. | | Supplier | The company providing the travel product: cruise line, tour operator, hotel chain, transfer company. | | TESS | D2M's CRM (customer relationship management) system for tracking clients, bookings, and commissions. | | Turnaround Day | The day one cruise ends and another begins in the same port. Crew cleans and restocks the ship. Passengers disembark in the morning, new passengers board in the afternoon. | | Upgrade | Moving a client to a higher cabin category at no or reduced cost. Often based on availability at check-in. | | Veranda | A private balcony attached to the cabin. Standard on most luxury ships. | | WAIVERS | D2M's automated client monitoring system that scans for port changes, schedule shifts, airline cancellations, and price drops. | | Waitlist | A booking placed on hold when the desired cabin category is sold out. If inventory opens, waitlisted bookings are filled in order. | | Wholesaler | A company that packages travel products (flights, hotels, transfers) at net rates for travel advisors (e.g., Centrav for flights, Hotelbeds for hotels, Kiwitaxi for transfers). | | World Cruise | A multi-month, round-the-world itinerary. Usually 90-150 days. High-ticket, high-commission, high-touch. |
