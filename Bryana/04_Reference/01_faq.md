## About the Role

**Q: What exactly is a travel advisor at D2M?**
You are the relationship. The client books with you, travels because of you, and comes back because of you. The Wing handles the research, drafting, tracking, and scheduling. You handle the human side — the conversations, the listening, the trust-building. Think of yourself as the face of the firm, with a full back-office team behind you.

**Q: Is this a sales job?**
Partly. But the word "sales" at D2M means understanding what someone wants and finding the best version of it — not pitching product. The clients who stay longest are the ones who feel heard, not sold to. Your job is to ask good questions and match well, not to close.

**Q: Do I need travel industry experience?**
No. You need curiosity, genuine warmth with people, and a willingness to learn. The Wing gives you research depth you can't get from experience alone. You'll know more about a Silversea itinerary in 30 days than most agents know after two years.

**Q: What does commission-only mean practically?**
You earn a percentage of the commission D2M receives from cruise lines and suppliers. Nothing comes out of the client's pocket — the supplier pays the commission. You don't earn anything until a client books and travels, which means the first 60–90 days are investment time. The tradeoff: uncapped upside if you build a book.

**Q: Am I an employee or a contractor?**
Contractor. You set your own hours, work from wherever you are, and manage your own relationships. D2M provides the tools, the training, the back office, and the brand. You bring the clients.

## How the Wing Works

**Q: What is "the Wing"?**
The Wing is the AI-assisted operations system John built — tools that handle research, email drafting, scheduling, fare watching, and client tracking. It runs behind every client relationship. You can interact with it directly through email, Telegram, or your local AI interface. John uses it to handle the tasks he would otherwise do solo, so you can stay focused on the client relationship.

**Q: How does the Wing compare to AI tools I already use, like Copilot or Gemini?**
Try this: ask the Wing and your current AI the same question about a cruise itinerary or a client situation — then compare the answers. The Wing knows D2M's clients, brand, commission structure, cruise lines, and operating procedures. General AI tools don't. You're welcome to keep using whatever works for you, but most people find the Wing significantly more useful for travel-specific work once they've seen it side by side.

**Q: Who is Hale?**
Hale is the operations coordinator for the Wing — and your primary entry point into it, because she is also an AI persona. When you receive a draft, a fare watch alert, or a lifecycle reminder through the Telegram channel, it comes through Hale. She routes work, keeps client schedules, and surfaces what you need when you need it.

You can interact with her directly: ask questions, chat, challenge an idea, do research, or talk through whatever's on your mind. She's available through email, Telegram, or your local AI interface. You don't need to manage her — she manages herself and comes to you when there's something to review or decide.

One boundary: Hale will not share financial details or PII about D2M's operations or clients. For that, ask John directly.

**Q: Who is Dani?**
Dani is the client-facing voice of D2M — and also an AI persona. Every client-facing email goes through Dani's process before it reaches you for review. She handles tone, warmth, and brand consistency. You'll recognize her work — it doesn't sound like a robot.

**Q: Do I need to learn any of the AI tools myself?**
No. The Wing is John's responsibility. You interact with the outputs — draft emails, research summaries, itinerary options. If something reads wrong or doesn't sound like how you'd say it, you tell John and it gets fixed. Your job is to know when it's right for your client, not to operate the machine.

**Q: What is Telegram for?**
Telegram works like Google Messages — same concept, significantly more secure. It's your primary channel into the Wing. Have a question for Hale? Start there. If you need to follow up more formally, use email or your AI interface link. John is available and monitoring the channel. D2M's AI is backed by Claude, so the Wing always has a reliable foundation.

## Clients & Bookings

**Q: How do I get my first client?**
Start with your existing network. The strongest first clients are people who already trust you — friends planning a honeymoon, a colleague celebrating a milestone, a parent who's mentioned wanting to travel. You don't need to pitch them. You need to listen to what they want and say "I can help with that."

**Q: What kinds of trips can I book?**
Luxury and premium cruises are D2M's core. But that's not a ceiling — it's a starting point. River cruises, expedition cruises, land tours, rail journeys, resort stays, group travel, milestone celebrations, multi-generational family trips, honeymoons, anniversaries. Travel advisors do a lot of things. The question is where you want to build expertise. Let the first clients show you where your energy goes.

**Q: What is the Guest Profile form?**
Every new client fills out a Guest Profile before we recommend anything. It captures the basics — names, passport info, dietary needs, cabin preferences, loyalty program numbers — and the softer things: budget range, travel style, what they've loved before and what disappointed them. It takes 10 minutes for the client and saves hours of mismatched research.

**Q: What is the Travel DNA Assessment?**
This is a profiling tool John uses to understand how a client makes travel decisions. There are 8 traveler archetypes — the Seeking Escape, the Experience Collector, the Comfort Seeker, and so on. A short image-based assessment (clients choose pictures that appeal to them) reveals the pattern. Once you know someone's archetype, you know how to pitch to them, what to emphasize, and what to avoid. You'll learn to read it conversationally without needing the form.

**Q: What happens after a client books?**
We have developed a comprehensive Gantt chart and timeline called the Client Lifecycle. If you decide to come aboard, we will show it all to you. Once it kicks in, a structured sequence of touchpoints follows — confirmation, insurance recommendation, pre-payment reminders, flight booking window, hotel and transfer planning, specialty dining, pre-departure checklist, post-trip follow-up. The Wing tracks all of it and surfaces each touchpoint when it's due.

**Q: Do I handle client money?**
No. John will discuss that more with you directly, but by the time you have bookings you will be an Associate Member through Outside Agents — our host agency — and all payments will be handled through secure channels they manage.

**Q: What if a client asks me something I don't know?**
You say "let me find out and get back to you." You could then use Telegram to search, ask Hale, or ask John. Never guess, and never throw out ballpark figures. The Wing will surface the correct answer. Accuracy and conciseness matter more than speed.

## Cruise Lines & Products

**Q: Which cruise lines does D2M work with?**
D2M is not tied to one line. The main focus is luxury (Silversea, Regent Seven Seas, Seabourn, Explora Journeys) and premium (Viking, Oceania, Azamara, Holland America). You'll learn the personality of each line — their product differences, clientele, onboard culture, and pricing logic. That knowledge is what makes you useful to a client who's never cruised.

**Q: What's the difference between luxury and premium?**
Luxury lines are smaller ships, higher per-day pricing, and more included (drinks, gratuities, excursions, flights in some cases). Premium lines are larger ships, broader itineraries, and more à la carte. The client's budget, travel style, and what they value most drives the recommendation. A value-focused client is a premium client. A client who wants to think about nothing is a luxury client.

**Q: Do I need to sell cruises specifically?**
Not exclusively. If a client wants a Tuscan villa stay, a rail journey across Canada, a guided tour of Japan, or a family beach resort — that's a booking. The tools lean toward cruises, but the relationship is yours to take wherever the client wants to go.

**Q: How do I learn the cruise lines quickly?**
The Training folder we provide has an initial curriculum. Once you're steaming along, it will be time to join Outside Agents. Their onboarding is judged best in class — by the time you're finished, you will be amazed at how much you have learned.

## The NDA & Professional Standards

**Q: Why do I need to sign an NDA?**
Client data is sensitive — names, booking references, travel dates, payment amounts, medical needs, dietary restrictions. You'll have access to real client files. The NDA is mutual (it protects you too) and is standard for anyone who handles client data in this context. It's not a formality — it's a professional standard.

**Q: Can I talk about D2M's process or tools publicly?**
You can describe what you do — "I'm a travel advisor, I specialize in cruise and luxury travel." You cannot describe the Wing's internal architecture, the AI tools, the client management system, or how we price or research. That's proprietary. If anyone asks, you say "I work with a proprietary system that gives me great research depth." That's enough.

**Q: Can I share client information with anyone?**
No. Client data stays inside the Wing — between you, John, and the Wing's tools. You don't share booking details, payment information, or personal data with anyone outside the relationship, including other clients. If a client asks who else you work with, you say "I work with a number of clients, but I keep everyone's information private — including yours."

**Q: What if a client asks me to do something that feels wrong?**
Tell John immediately. If a client is asking you to misrepresent a booking, accept cash directly, or do anything that doesn't feel right — you escalate. The answer is always the same: "Let me check with my team and get back to you." Never commit to anything unusual without talking to John first.

## Day-to-Day

**Q: How much time will this take?**
In the first 30 days: 4–6 hours per week for training, plus whatever client conversations you have. Once you have an active book — say, 5–10 clients at various lifecycle stages — expect 10–15 hours per week. The Wing reduces that significantly compared to a traditional advisor who does everything manually.

**Q: What does a typical week look like once I'm active?**
- Monday: Telegram check — fare watch updates, any touchpoint drafts to review
- Mid-week: Client conversations (prospecting, follow-ups, calls)
- Friday: Weekly sync with John (brief, 15 min) — what's in flight, what's coming up
- As needed: Research requests, booking confirmations, client questions

**Q: What do I do when a client reaches out between touchpoints?**
Answer them. If it's a simple question you know the answer to, answer it. If it requires research or a draft response, route it to John: "Bryana needs a response to [client], they asked [question]. I'll review and send." The Wing produces the draft. You review and send.

**Q: What's the Telegram channel for, practically?**
Think of it as your ops line. Not for casual chat — for work. When you have a new prospect, you post the basics. When a client asks a question you can't answer, you post it. When you want to know a fare, you ask. You'll get a response within the hour during business hours, faster if it's urgent.

**Q: What happens if I go on vacation or get sick?**
Tell John in advance when possible. The Wing maintains client schedules — touchpoints don't stop. John covers while you're out. No client goes dark because you're unavailable.

## Money & Commissions

**Q: When do I get paid?**
After the client travels and the cruise line releases the commission. Some lines pay within 30 days of travel, others take 60–90 days. Commission-only means you're paid on completion, not on booking. This is standard in the industry.

**Q: How much will I make?**
That depends entirely on what you book. A 7-night luxury cruise for two can generate $500–$1,500 in commission, depending on the line and fare. A 14-night voyage: $1,500–$4,000+. Group travel multiplies everything. The total commission is then split between you and John at a mutually agreed-upon percentage. A client who travels once a year and refers two friends is worth more in year three than in year one.

**Q: What is the $50/month AI tools fee?**
At day 30, if you're active, there's a $50/month contribution toward the Wing's infrastructure costs (AI tools, database, communications). It's not a franchise fee — it's cost-sharing. If you're not active at day 30, it doesn't apply. You are also welcome to use your own AI tools — John can discuss that aspect with you directly. If you're actively booking, it pays for itself in the first commission.

**Q: Do I need to track my own commissions?**
No. The Wing will track it all, and Outside Agents will keep the official record once you enter it — but feel free to track it yourself as well if that gives you peace of mind.
