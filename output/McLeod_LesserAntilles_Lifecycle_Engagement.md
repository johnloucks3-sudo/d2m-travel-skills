### TP 1.1 — Lesser Antilles Voyage Preview
**Send Date:** May 15, 2026
**To:** emcleod@gmail.com
**CC:** memcglas@gmail.com
**Subject:** Your Lesser Antilles — Five Islands, One Unforgettable Week
**WF-17:** Required — A2 Dembe research, A6 Luna narrative

Erik, Melissa —

Seven months out. Here's a preview of the five islands waiting for you.

---

**CHARLOTTE AMALIE, ST. THOMAS (USVI)**
The gateway island. A natural deep-water harbor surrounded by green hills, duty-free shopping along Main Street (Dronningens Gade), and some of the best views in the Caribbean from the mountaintop at Drake's Seat. Magens Bay — consistently rated one of the world's top ten beaches — is a 20-minute taxi ride from the pier.

The food scene has evolved. Gladys' Café in the old Danish warehouse district serves the best local cuisine on the island — curry goat, johnnycakes, saltfish. For something upscale, The Old Stone Farmhouse in Estate Lovenlund is a restored 18th-century sugar plantation.

**ROSEAU, DOMINICA**
The nature island. Dominica is what the Caribbean looked like before tourism. No mega-resorts. Dense rainforest, volcanic hot springs, and Trafalgar Falls — twin waterfalls cascading into a gorge. The Champagne Reef off the southwest coast has volcanic vents that release warm bubbles — snorkeling through champagne-like water.

The Waitukubuli National Trail is the Caribbean's longest hiking trail. You won't do all 115 miles, but the Segment 1 trailhead starts near Scotts Head — a moderate 3-hour coastal hike with views of both the Atlantic and Caribbean simultaneously.

For food: The Riverside Café in Roseau for Creole lunch. Local ingredients, river-table setting.

**ST. JOHN'S, ANTIGUA & BARBUDA**
365 beaches — one for every day of the year. That's the claim, and it's roughly accurate. The standout is Half Moon Bay on the Atlantic side — dramatic coral reef, wild surf, nearly empty on weekdays. Dickenson Bay on the northwest coast is the calm, resort-beach counterpart.

Nelson's Dockyard in English Harbour is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the only continuously working Georgian-era dockyard in the world. The Admiral's Inn serves lunch overlooking the harbor. Shirley Heights lookout above the dockyard offers the panoramic view that defines Antigua.

**BASSETERRE, ST. KITTS & NEVIS**
The quieter jewel. Brimstone Hill Fortress is the standout — a UNESCO-listed 17th-century citadel perched 800 feet above the Caribbean. The views from the fortress walls are among the best in the Eastern Caribbean.

The St. Kitts Scenic Railway circles the island on old sugar-plantation narrow-gauge track. It's touristy but genuinely enjoyable — 18 miles along the coast with open-air observation cars and rum punch service. Romney Manor and the Caribelle Batik workshop are worth a stop.

**TORTOLA, BRITISH VIRGIN ISLANDS**
The sailing capital of the Caribbean. Cane Garden Bay has the white sand and the beach bars. The Baths on neighboring Virgin Gorda — massive granite boulders forming pools and grottos at water's edge — are reachable by ferry or excursion. It's the #1 photo opportunity in the BVI.

Pusser's Road Town Pub serves their proprietary Painkiller cocktail — rum, coconut cream, orange juice, pineapple, nutmeg. It's the signature drink of the BVI and tastes exactly like a Caribbean afternoon should.

---

**CHRISTMAS AT SEA**
December 25 falls during your at-sea days. Regent does Christmas aboard with elegance — special menus in Compass Rose, decorations throughout the ship, holiday entertainment in the Constellation Theatre. It's one of the most peaceful ways to spend the holiday.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Five islands, five distinct personalities. From the nature wilderness of Dominica to the beach culture of Antigua, from the history of St. Kitts to the sailing paradise of Tortola. And Christmas on the open Caribbean. This is going to be special.
