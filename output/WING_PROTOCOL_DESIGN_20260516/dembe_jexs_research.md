## BLUF

Joint exercise doctrine (JELC via CJCSM 3500.03F) provides a five-phase planning and execution model — Design, Planning, Preparation, Execution, and Evaluation/Analysis/Reports — that maps with reasonable precision to the Commander's proposed seven-step Wing protocol, though the analogy compresses significantly due to D2M's operational tempo: what joint forces take 12-24 months to plan, the Wing executes in minutes to hours. The doctrinal AAR is genuinely powerful but only works if lessons are codified into updated SOPs/doctrine, not just discussed — the military's institutional failure mode (CALL archives that nobody reads) is a direct risk the Wing should explicitly design against. Three elements of joint doctrine translate cleanly: the hotwash-then-formal-AAR sequence, the "sustain/improve" framing, and the principle that every exercise outcome must update the next exercise's design; one element requires heavy adaptation (the 12-24 month JELC timeline); and one should be discarded entirely (the observer/controller evaluation bureaucracy, which is appropriate for brigade-level exercises, not a nine-person AI wing).

## SECTION 1: JEXS PHASE MAP

### Background: JOPES vs. APEX vs. JELC — Sorting the Terminology

**JOPES** (Joint Operation Planning and Execution System) was the legacy DOD C2 planning system governing how combatant commands developed contingency and crisis action plans. It has been formally superseded by **APEX** (Adaptive Planning and Execution Enterprise), which itself has been superseded in JP 5-0 (December 2020) by the updated construct simply called **Joint Planning**. JOPES/APEX terminology persists in the force because doctrine changes faster than vocabulary.

**JEXS** (Joint Exercise and Training System) is the functional system within which JELC operates. The canonical reference for joint exercise planning is **CJCSM 3500.03F** — the Joint Training Manual for the Armed Forces of the United States. The operative framework is the **Joint Event Life Cycle (JELC)**.

Sources: [CJCSM 3500.03F — Joint Training Manual](https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Library/Manuals/CJCSM%203500.03F.pdf) | [JP 5-0, Joint Planning (2020)](https://www.jcs.mil/Doctrine/DOCNET/JP-5-0-Joint-Planning/) | [CJCS Guide 3501 — The Joint Training System](https://www.indianstrategicknowledgeonline.com/web/g3501.pdf)

## SECTION 2: AAR DOCTRINE — THE CANONICAL FORMAT

### What an AAR Is (and Is Not)

An AAR is **not a critique, not a debrief, and not an evaluation**. It is a structured, professional, candid discussion in which participants self-discover what happened and why. The facilitator does not teach or critique — the facilitator asks questions that cause the group to surface their own understanding. Blame and rank are operationally suppressed. Source: [FM 25-101 Appendix G — After Action Reviews, GlobalSecurity.org](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/policy/army/fm/25-101/fm251_13.htm)

### The Four Canonical AAR Questions

These are doctrinal. All four must be answered:

1. **What was planned / intended to happen?** (Establishes the baseline — what the standard was)
2. **What actually happened?** (Establishes the observation — what the unit did relative to standard)
3. **Why was there a gap?** (Root cause analysis — what factors drove the variance)
4. **What will we sustain or improve?** (Forward-looking action — specific behaviors or procedures to repeat or change)

Source: [After-Action Review — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After-action_review) | [FM 7-0 Appendix K — After Action Reviews](https://www.first.army.mil/Portals/102/FM%207-0%20Appendix%20K.pdf) | [CALL Handbook 11-33 Appendix C](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/report/call/call_11-33-appc.htm)

### Formal vs. Informal AAR

| Type | Timing | Format | Who Facilitates |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Informal (Hotwash)** | Immediately post-execution | Verbal, field-side, no documentation required | Team leader or first-line supervisor |
| **Formal AAR** | Hours to days post-execution | Structured discussion, written documentation | Trained AAR conductor (neutral facilitator preferred) |
| **Written AAR Report** | Days to weeks post-execution | Observation-Discussion-Recommendation format, submitted to CALL | Staff officer or designated recorder |

### How Lessons Are Supposed to Propagate

The doctrine is explicit: lessons must be **codified, not just discussed**. The CALL (Center for Army Lessons Learned) model:

1. Verbal discussion (hotwash) — immediate, tactical
2. Written AAR — observation-discussion-recommendation format, specific and actionable
3. Submission to institutional archive (CALL database) — accessible to units preparing for similar operations
4. Incorporation into **TTP (Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures)** — the lesson becomes doctrine for future operations
5. The **next exercise design is updated** based on prior AAR findings — the MSEL for the following JELC cycle explicitly addresses previous shortfalls

**The institutional failure mode:** The military's known weak link is Steps 3-5. Units conduct excellent hotwashes and AARs but the lessons go into a CALL database that deploying units often do not access. The lesson exists but the propagation chain breaks at the institutional level. Source: [CALL Handbook 11-33 — Establishing a Lessons Learned Program](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/report/call/call_11-33-appc.htm)

**What makes lessons stick (doctrine-derived):**
- **Specificity over generalization** — "Sergeant Johnson's Bradley hesitated at Grid 441 because the comms check skipped the radio relay node" propagates. "Communication was an issue" does not.
- **Timeliness** — immediate hotwash captures what formal AAR misses
- **Accessibility** — lessons must be findable by the next team before they need them, not after
- **Mandatory integration** — the next exercise design must explicitly reference prior AAR findings (this is the step most organizations skip)

## SECTION 4: TRANSLATION ASSESSMENT

### What Maps Cleanly (Adopt as-is)

**1. The Prompt = Training Intent.**
The Commander's instinct to treat every operational prompt as a discrete training event with an intent statement is doctrinally sound. Military commanders write training intent before every exercise. D2M should formalize this: every Wing task should carry a one-line statement of what "good" looks like before execution begins.

**2. The Hotwash = Immediate Post-Execution Debrief.**
The military distinction between hotwash (immediate, verbal, informal) and formal AAR (structured, written, delayed) is exactly right and the Commander's instinct to include it is correct. The hotwash must happen within 60 minutes of action completion. Its outputs are fast and tactical, not systemic.

**3. Sustain / Improve Framing.**
The doctrinal "what to sustain, what to improve" question structure is directly usable in D2M. It is superior to "what went wrong" because it forces positive identification of what worked, which prevents discarding successful practices that weren't consciously recognized.

**4. The Feedback Loop: AAR Output Feeds Next Prompt.**
This is the most important doctrinal principle. Every AAR in doctrine explicitly informs the design of the next exercise. For D2M: every hotwash / document improvement must identify at least one change to CLAUDE.md, persona files, or Wing protocols — not just a note, but a committed, dated update. If the lesson doesn't change a document, it didn't propagate.

### What Requires Adaptation (Modify for D2M tempo)

**1. Timeline Compression.**
The JELC runs 12-24 months for major joint exercises. D2M runs the equivalent cycle in minutes to hours. The doctrinal conferences (IPC, MPC, FPC) exist to coordinate across dozens of organizations. D2M's equivalent is the internal Wing staff discussion and HALE synthesis, which must be compressed without losing the substance of those conferences: scope alignment, conflict identification, and final go/no-go. The solution is **explicit timestamps** on each step, not physical conferences.

**2. The "Observer/Controller" Role.**
Joint exercises assign dedicated O/Cs (observer/controllers) who watch the training audience and provide objective evaluation. D2M has no dedicated O/C. The closest analog is HALE's synthesis function and the Commander's own review. For critical Wing tasks, consider designating one staff persona as explicit "observer" — not participating in the task, but watching the process and reporting to the hotwash.

**3. Written AAR vs. Document Improvement.**
"Document improvement" is a lighter touch than a written AAR. For routine Wing tasks, document improvement is sufficient. For Gate 4 decisions, failed client actions, or any task that produced a significant surprise, the Wing should conduct a full written AAR using the four canonical questions and submit it as a dated entry in `hale_decisions.md`.

**4. The "Training Objective" Construct.**
Joint exercises always begin with defined Training Objectives (TOs): specific, measurable tasks the exercise is designed to train. D2M currently operates without explicit TOs on most Wing actions. Adapting this: every non-trivial Wing task should have a stated success criterion before execution begins (not after). HALE should enforce this as a pre-execution gate.

### What to Discard (Leave Behind)

**1. The JTIMS Database / CALL Submission Model.**
The military's institutional lesson archive (CALL database, JTIMS system) is designed for an organization of millions. D2M's "database" is `hale_decisions.md` and updated persona/protocol files. A separate lessons-learned database is overkill. The Wing's lesson propagation model must be simpler and more direct: lesson identified → file updated → version controlled → next session inherits the change.

**2. Multi-Day Planning Conferences.**
The IPC/MPC/FPC structure exists to coordinate across time zones, services, and nations. The Wing operates in a single virtual space. Conferences become asynchronous staff discussion that HALE mediates and synthesizes. No ceremonial conference structure needed.

**3. The Observer/Controller Evaluation Bureaucracy.**
Joint exercises build elaborate O/C teams with evaluation matrices, TPO forms, and formal observation protocols. This bureaucracy is load-bearing when you're training 5,000 soldiers but irrelevant for nine AI personas and one Commander. Keep the *principle* (objective observation of process) but discard the apparatus.

**4. Sequential Phase Rigidity.**
JOPES' original sequential planning phases were specifically cited as a weakness APEX was designed to correct. D2M should not introduce sequential phase rigidity — the Wing's strength is adaptive, parallel execution. Doctrinal phases inform the *structure* of Wing operations, not a mandatory sequential progression.

## SECTION 5: RECOMMENDATIONS

Three confidence levels applied: HIGH (strong doctrinal backing), MODERATE (requires Wing-specific validation), LOW (speculative, flag for Commander).

### REC 1 — Formalize the Pre-Execution Intent Statement (HIGH confidence)
Before any non-trivial Wing task executes, the tasking message (prompt) should include a one-sentence statement of success criteria: "This task is complete when X." This is the Training Objective adapted for D2M tempo. HALE enforces it at intake. If the prompt arrives without it, HALE asks once and documents the response. This eliminates the most common failure mode: executing a task correctly against the wrong definition of "done."

**Implementation:** Add a "Success Criterion" field to Wing task intake. Hale documents it in the task log. Trivial tasks (file reads, status checks) exempt.

### REC 2 — Institutionalize the Two-Tier Review: Hotwash at 60 Minutes, AAR at 24 Hours (HIGH confidence)
The military's hotwash/AAR distinction is directly applicable. D2M should adopt it explicitly:

- **Hotwash (T+60 minutes):** HALE records four items — what was intended, what happened, what to sustain, one immediate fix. Verbally surfaced to Commander if action-driven, logged in `hale_decisions.md` regardless.
- **Formal AAR (T+24 hours, for significant actions only):** Four canonical questions answered in writing. At least one CLAUDE.md, persona file, or protocol file updated as a direct result. Git commit required — the lesson must be version-controlled to exist.

**Trigger for formal AAR:** Any client-facing action, any Gate 1-4 decision, any action that produced a surprise (positive or negative). Routine operational tasks use hotwash only.

### REC 3 — Close the Propagation Loop: Every AAR Must Touch a File (HIGH confidence)
This is the most important structural change. The military's known failure mode is lessons that enter archives and never resurface. D2M must design against this explicitly. The doctrinal principle is: **AAR output feeds the next exercise's Design Phase.**

D2M translation: Every formal AAR must produce at least one committed change to an operational file (CLAUDE.md, persona file, skill file, or documented protocol). If no file changes, the lesson did not propagate — it was filed and forgotten. HALE is responsible for identifying the target file and staging the edit. Commander approves if it touches CLAUDE.md or persona files.

**Metric:** HALE tracks "AAR-to-file-commit ratio." Target: 100%. A zero-commit AAR is a failed AAR.

### REC 4 — Add a "Wargame" Step Between Staff Discussion and HALE Synthesis (MODERATE confidence)
Current Commander sequence: Prompt → Staff Discussion → HALE Synthesis. Doctrinal planning includes a **wargaming step** between COA development and synthesis — explicitly asking "what could go wrong, and how do we handle it?" The Wing currently reaches synthesis without this adversarial check.

**D2M adaptation:** After Staff Discussion, HALE performs a one-turn "red team" scan — identifies the most likely failure mode, the most consequential failure mode, and whether there is a mitigation. Adds approximately 90 seconds of processing. Prevents the class of surprises that are, by definition, predictable in advance. If the answer is "no significant failure modes," log that and proceed.

**Implementation:** HALE standing protocol, no Commander action required. A5 Castillo or A2 Dembe as designated red-teamer depending on task type.

### REC 5 — Define "Wing Exercise" Cadence: Periodic Deliberate Review (MODERATE confidence)
Joint doctrine distinguishes between **Crisis Action Planning** (reactive, time-pressured, adapts to the environment) and **Deliberate Planning** (proactive, unhurried, anticipates future scenarios). D2M currently operates almost entirely in Crisis Action mode — reacting to client needs, Commander directives, and operational pressures.

The recommendation is a monthly **Wing Deliberate Review** — not a daily operations review, but a structured look at what the Wing would do if a specific scenario arose (a new client type, a supplier failure, a booking crisis). This is the D2M equivalent of the deliberate planning cycle. It ensures the Wing has thought through scenarios before they become crises. Estimated time: 90 minutes per month. Output: one updated protocol or one new SOP. Owner: A5 Castillo as Operating Tempo Owner.

**Note:** This recommendation is lower confidence because it requires Commander buy-in on the time investment and because D2M's operational volume may make monthly deliberate review feel redundant with existing ops tempo. Surface for Commander decision — this is a Gate 4 consideration.

## INFORMATION GAPS

The following gaps are explicitly flagged. Gaps matter as much as what I found.

1. **JTIMS integration detail:** The Joint Training Information Management System is the doctrinal repository for exercise evaluation data. I was unable to access JTIMS-specific documentation to determine whether its lesson propagation model offers any further insight for D2M's `hale_decisions.md` architecture. Low priority gap — D2M's system is simpler and purpose-fit.

2. **HOW the military measures "lesson propagation success":** Doctrine says lessons must reach future units. I found no quantitative metric for how the military measures whether that actually happened. This is a known institutional weakness. D2M's Rec 3 (AAR-to-file-commit ratio) is original design, not doctrine-derived.

3. **JEXS as a distinct system from JELC:** The term "JEXS" appears in Commander's directive. In current doctrine, the functional system is the Joint Training System (JTS) governed by CJCSM 3500.03F. "JEXS" may be older terminology or a specific sub-system. I found no standalone JEXS documentation as a discrete system separate from the broader JTS/JELC framework. Assessment: JEXS and JELC are effectively synonymous for D2M planning purposes. Confidence: MODERATE.
