### PANAMA CITY OVERVIEW

**The Port Experience:**
Panama City sits at the Caribbean entrance to the Canal. Your ship docks at the Amador Causeway (formerly Fort Amador) — a secure, walkable area with shops, restaurants, and canal views. This is the safest, most accessible part of the city for cruise passengers.

**Historical Context:**
- **Founded 1519** — Spanish colonial hub; center of colonial trade routes
- **Casco Viejo (Old Town)** — UNESCO World Heritage district; narrow streets, plazas, churches, museums
- **Panama Canal (opened 1914)** — Revolutionized global shipping; remains one of humanity's greatest engineering achievements
- **Modern Panama City** — Gleaming skyline with luxury hotels, casinos, restaurants; economic hub of Central America

**Top 5 Must-See Attractions (Walking Distance or Easy Taxi):**
1. **Gatun Locks Transit** — Watch your ship navigate the locks. If not included in cruise, visible from port; explains canal mechanics. [Canal Authority Live Cams](https://www.pancanal.com/en/general-information/live-web-cameras/) / On-ship announcement for transit timing.
2. **Casco Viejo (Old Town)** — Colonial plazas, cathedrals, museums. 30-min walk from port OR 15-min Uber ($5–8). Start at Plaza Mayor, grab coffee, wander. Allow 2–3 hours.
3. **Amador Causeway** — Walking/jogging path connecting four islands; restaurants, shops, views of Bridge of the Americas. Directly from port; free to explore.
4. **Panama Viejo (Panama la Vieja)** — Original 1519 settlement ruins (UNESCO site). 15-min Uber from port ($8–10). Museum on-site explains colonial history. 1–2 hours.
5. **Biomuseo (Biodiversity Museum)** — Frank Gehry–designed; documents Central American ecosystems. Amador Causeway area; walkable. 1.5–2 hours.

### DINING & LOCAL FLAVORS

**Signature Panamanian Dishes to Try:**
- **Ceviche** — Fresh fish "cooked" in lime juice, onion, cilantro; served cold. Quintessential.
- **Sancocho** — Hearty stew (plantain, yam, chicken or beef). Warming, authentic.
- **Empanadas** — Fried pastries filled with meat, cheese, or plantain. Ubiquitous street food.
- **Patacones** — Fried plantain rounds, served twice-fried. Comfort food; pairs with everything.
- **Ropa Vieja** — Slow-cooked beef with peppers and tomatoes. Cuban-influenced; excellent.

### LOCAL CUSTOMS & TIPS

| Topic | What You Should Know |
|-------|---------------------|
| **Tipping** | 10–15% at restaurants (included in bill at high-end places; ask). Taxi drivers: round up or 5%. Tour guides: $5–10. |
| **Photography** | Generally fine in public areas. Ask before photographing people in markets. No photos in churches without permission. |
| **Dress Code** | Casual dress fine for daytime. Restaurants require closed-toe shoes in evenings (locals dress up). |
| **Bargaining** | Market haggling is expected (10–20% negotiable). Shops: fixed prices. |
| **Water Safety** | Tap water is safe to drink in tourist areas. Ocean: Calm in Gatun Lake (fresh water); Atlantic coast has strong currents — check conditions. |
| **Holidays/Closures** | Most shops/museums open 9 AM–6 PM. Some close for siesta 12–1 PM. Churches keep irregular hours. |
| **Greetings** | Handshake is standard. "Buenos días" / "Buenas tardes" / "Buenas noches" are used. Friendly but formal until invited otherwise. |
| **Electricity/Power** | Same as US (110V, flat plugs). No adaptor needed. |

### LOCAL GEMS (Not Standard Tour-Operator Stuff)

**Amador Causeway Stroll** — 1–2 hour walk from port. Ocean views, local restaurants, sunset. Free. Safer than downtown at dusk.

**El Mercado de Mariscos (Fish Market)** — Authentic, loud, fresh. Pick your fish, have the vendor cook it. Lunch under awning. ~$12/person for whole grilled snapper. Negotiable but always a deal.

**Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute Visitor Center** — Small, excellent exhibits on Central American biology. Quieter than Biomuseo. Free entry, $3 donation suggested. 30 min from port.

### CURRENCY & PAYMENT

| Topic | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Official Currency** | Panamanian Balboa (PAB); 1 Balboa = 1 USD. USD accepted everywhere. |
| **Exchange Rate** | No need to exchange; use USD directly. |
| **ATMs** | Available at port shops and throughout city. Withdraw USD. No fees for US bank cards. |
| **Credit Cards** | Visa/Mastercard accepted everywhere. American Express less common; bring backup. |
| **Tipping** | 10–15% at restaurants (often included in bill). Taxi: round up. Tour guide: $5–10. |
| **Cash vs. Card** | Markets, small vendors: cash. Restaurants, shops: either. Bring ~$100 USD cash for small vendors. |
| **What NOT to Pay For** | Water (tap is safe), tip jar at port shops (not required), unsolicited "guides" (often overpriced). |

## USAGE NOTES FOR TEAM

✅ **What to Do:**
- Copy template
- Research destination + client profile
- Fill in real excursion links + current pricing
- Gather 3–5 guest testimonials (actual guest feedback, not fabricated)
- Include 1–2 personal photos or ship photos (authentic, labeled)
- Add D2M Concierge Picks matched to client vibe (adventure? culture? food? relaxation?)

❌ **What NOT to Do:**
- Generic descriptions (be specific: names, prices, testimonials, links)
- Broken links (verify every URL works before sending)
- Outdated info (check currency, holiday closures, boat schedules before send)
- Unverified restaurant recommendations (eat there or read current reviews first)
- Stock photos without attribution (use ship photos, real excursion images, or properly licensed stock)
