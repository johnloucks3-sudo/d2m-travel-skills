## THE CYCLE: HOW WE BUILD

There's a rhythm to how John and Claude work together. It's not "tell the AI what to do." It's a partnership with a very specific cadence:

**1. Yoda Dreams It**
John has a vision — sometimes a full architecture, sometimes a sentence. "I want my staff to hear everything." "What if I could star an email and it just... does the thing?" "Build me a dining pipeline — that's where the trust is." The ideas come from 30 years of military leadership and a year of watching how travel advisors actually work.

**2. We Discuss It**
This is where the idea gets pressure-tested. John describes the intent. Claude asks the hard questions: What does "hear everything" mean technically? Which star colors does Gmail actually support? What's the minimum viable version? John pushes back, refines, sometimes changes direction entirely. The discussion is fast — multiple-choice questions, not essays.

**3. Claude Validates It**
Before a single line of code, Claude reads the existing codebase, checks for conflicts, identifies dependencies, and briefs John on what's about to change. "Here's what I'm going to modify, here's the risk, here's the approach." John approves or redirects. No surprises.

**4. Claude Builds and Tests It**
Full function-level code. Not patches — complete, working modules. Every new feature is syntax-checked, import-tested, and run against real data before it's declared done. If a Groq API call times out, we retry. If a Gmail search returns wrong results, we debug the query. Nothing ships untested.

**5. We Do Real Work With It**
This is the part that matters. Within hours of building the dining curation pipeline, we rendered a real Stockholm restaurant guide for the Furlow party. Within minutes of building the star protocol, we processed 4 real starred emails. The commission extraction tool was immediately used to audit $8,300 in take-home commission across 9 active bookings. The tools earn their keep on day one.
