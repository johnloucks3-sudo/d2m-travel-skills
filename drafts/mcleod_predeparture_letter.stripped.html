Erik and Melissa,

In a few days the Mediterranean is yours. Rome at first light, the Forum yours alone before the crowds wake it. A Florence morning, espresso bitter on the tongue, the Duomo going gold as the sun clears the hills. Then the Silver Muse, and that particular blue you only get out there. And at the end, Venice — the lagoon opening as the ship draws in, the whole city rising out of the water to meet you. That’s your trip. That’s what we’ve been carving — yours, piece by piece, from a heap of receipts and fine print into one shaped, certain thing with nothing rough left in it.

You’ve already felt the sculpting hand — and also seen the gaps. The transfers Silversea won’t cover for your early Rome arrival and late Venice exit, the extra air fee — some you hit alongside us and we worked through together; others we caught first and brought you the options. Nothing blindsided you. That’s the job: see it coming, put it in front of you early, sort it together.

Here’s how the work goes, since you’ve watched me do it. I bring the vision and keep refining it down to specs; Dani and the staff craft to those specs — the sanding, the polishing, the buffing to a lustre — and we work it back and forth until nothing catches the hand. The judgment stays mine. You get the trip. And lately I’ve found those same hands don’t only shape a journey: when someone brings me the harder stuff — an immigration tangle, a friend who lost a spouse right before a cruise, a couple trying to get their daughters to Japan — we start carving again, on something that has nothing to do with travel, and none of it for money. The business is quietly becoming something I don’t have a name for yet. Honestly, that still amazes me — that the dreaded, tangled parts of a life can be handled down into something a person can carry.

There’s one more thing I want to hand you, and it’s not the itinerary. It’s Reverie — a travel companion my staff built, then carved and buffed the same way, made for this voyage and the choices the two of you made. Your whole journey in your pocket, day by day: where you are, the port ahead, your own reservations, no digging through emails. Dani right there to answer anything, anytime. And a journal, to hold the days as they happen. The honest part, since you’ll want to know how it works: Reverie is wired to our servers here in Colorado — so the moment you jot a thought, it reaches us directly, nothing lost in a notebook.

Which brings me to the favor — and Melissa, this one starts with you. The document you built planning this trip was so meticulous, so well thought through, that we adopted your format into how we organize every client’s dossier now. You didn’t just receive the craft; you taught us some of it. So I’m asking you both to do it once more: while you travel, notice us. Three lines, whenever something pings:

•  Something that delighted you.
•  Something you wished we’d done.
•  A moment that felt rough.

Don’t spare me. Show me where we left it rough — the rough spots are how this gets better, and you’ve already made us better once. Keep the three notes in Reverie; wired home to Colorado, the moment you write one it’s already with us.
