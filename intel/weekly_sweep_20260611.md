## SECTION 1 — FUEL / FARE ENVIRONMENT (PRIORITY ITEM)

### Iran/Hormuz: Current Status and Fare Timing Implications

**SITUATION (as of 2026-06-11):**
- The U.S.-Israel conflict with Iran (began 28 FEB 2026) caused Iran to close the Strait of Hormuz, triggering what the IEA called the "largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market."
- Brent crude surged ~35% peak to ~$80–82/bbl by 2 MAR. Has since pulled back ~20% from peak to ~$92.56/bbl as ceasefire optimism grew — **still elevated.**
- A U.S.-Iran ceasefire was declared 8 APR 2026. A 60-day MOU extension has been negotiated, but **Trump has not yet approved it.** The White House disputed Iran's characterization of the terms.
- Al Jazeera reports (10 APR): shipping through Hormuz remains "at a trickle" despite ceasefire. ~230 loaded oil tankers still waiting inside the Gulf.

**BOTTOM LINE FOR D2M FARE TIMING:**
- Air fares are up hard: summer 2026 international fares are 22–45% above last year on trans-Atlantic routes (Milan +38%, London +45%, Paris/Rome +22%+). Fuel surcharges are being reviewed upward every 2–4 weeks.
- **If the MOU is approved and Hormuz shipping resumes at pre-war levels, a meaningful fare pullback is possible — but not guaranteed, and not yet.** Trump approval is the trigger event to watch.
- For Commander's active clients (Furlow/Nichols/Ely Scandinavia Aug 29; Lyons Athens Aug 11): air windows are open now. **Given current elevated fares and no confirmed Hormuz resolution, waiting for a fuel-driven fare drop is a gamble.** The risk of further surcharge hikes within 2–4 weeks is real. Lock fares now or accept that risk with eyes open.
- **Twitter/Grok angle:** Aviation Twitter and energy desks are watching Trump's MOU decision as the binary event. If approved, expect loud "fares will drop" narrative — but hedging delays mean airline cost relief will lag 6–8 weeks behind oil prices. Passenger fare relief is slower than oil headlines suggest.
