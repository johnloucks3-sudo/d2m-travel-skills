# A Advisor’s Guide: Finding and Booking Independent Shore Excursions

## 1. Where to Source the Best Tours

To save your travelers 35% to 50% while giving them a far better experience, you should look beyond the cruise line's brochure. Here are the four best places to find high-quality tours:

*   **ToursByLocals**: This is our first choice for senior travelers. You can book a private local guide who has their own vehicle and driver. Because the tour is private, it can be customized completely for comfort and speed.
*   **Local Destination Management Companies (DMCs)**: These are local travel offices located in the port cities. If you establish a direct relationship with them, they can design custom tours. Since there is no middleman, their prices are often half of what the cruise ship charges for the same journey.
*   **Viator and GetYourGuide**: These are large online marketplaces. They are very useful for comparing prices and reading honest reviews from other travelers. You can easily filter for "private tours" to keep your travelers away from large, noisy crowds.

---

## 2. How to Vet Quality and Accessibility for Senior Travelers

Senior travelers deserve extra care to make sure they feel safe, respected, and comfortable. Do not rely on standard descriptions. Always send a message to the tour company and ask these exact questions:

*   **"Is there a separate driver and guide?"** 
    For seniors, this is very important. A separate driver can drop your travelers off right at the entrance of a historic site, and then go find parking. The guide can stay with your travelers so they never have to walk long distances across hot asphalt or steep parking lots.
*   **"What kind of vehicle is used, and how high is the step to get inside?"**
    Low-entry sedans or modern minivans with running boards are much easier to enter than high SUVs or large buses. Ensure the vehicle has working air conditioning.
*   **"Can we adjust the pace of the walking?"**
    A good private tour should have no rushed schedules. Ask the guide to plan for regular rest stops, shade, and accessible restrooms. Avoid places with steep cobblestones or long flights of stairs unless there is an elevator or a ramp.
*   **"Is there a secure place for personal items?"**
    Your travelers should feel safe leaving a small bag or a light jacket in the locked vehicle with the driver while they are sightseeing.

## 3. Timing and the Booking Window

To get the best guides and the best prices, timing is very important.

*   **The Cruise Window**: Cruise lines usually open their bookings 120 to 180 days before sailing. 
*   **Your Action Window**: You should begin researching and booking independent tours **90 to 120 days before the cruise departures**. 
*   **Why this works**: This window gives you enough time to see what the cruise line is offering, compare prices, and secure the very best local guides before their calendars fill up. The most patient and experienced guides always book up first.

---

## 4. Managing Risks Wisely

It is natural for travelers to feel a bit nervous about stepping off the ship with an independent guide. You can make them feel completely secure by addressing these three areas:

### Risk 1: Missing the Ship
This is the biggest worry. You can prevent this completely with the **Two-Hour Buffer Rule**.
*   **The Rule**: Always schedule the tour to end and return your travelers to the pier at least **two hours** before the official "all-aboard" time. 
*   **The Guarantee**: Use companies that offer an explicit "Back-to-Ship Guarantee." Reputable operators will put it in writing that if there is a delay, they will pay for the travelers' transportation and lodging to meet the ship at the next port.

### Risk 2: Cancellations and Refunds
Sometimes weather or rough seas keep a ship from docking.
*   **The Rule**: Only book with operators who offer a full refund if the ship cannot make it into the port. Viator, GetYourGuide, and professional local DMCs almost always have this policy, but you should always double-check and get it in writing.

### Risk 3: Travel Insurance
*   **The Rule**: Make sure your travelers have a good, comprehensive travel insurance policy. It should cover trip interruptions and missed connections. Having this protection gives everyone peace of mind.

## 5. Step-by-Step Port Workflow for the Advisor

You can follow these six simple steps for every port on your client's cruise itinerary:

1.  **Check the Port Details**: Look at the cruise schedule. Note the exact arrival and departure times. Check if the ship docks at a pier or if it uses "tender boats" to get people ashore. (If it uses tender boats, add an extra hour to your morning timeline).
2.  **Look at the Cruise Line’s Offerings**: Check what the ship is selling and what they are charging. Use this as your benchmark for price and quality.
3.  **Find the Alternative**: Search ToursByLocals or contact a trusted local DMC for a similar private tour. Aim for a price that is 35% to 50% lower, or a tour that includes much more personal attention for the same price.
4.  **Ask the Vet Questions**: Message the local guide. Ask about the vehicle step-in height, separate driver, walking terrain, and restroom availability.
5.  **Build the Safe Timeline**: Create a clear schedule. Ensure the tour starts 1 hour after arrival and finishes 2 hours before departure.
6.  **Create the Client Care Package**: Print a clear, simple itinerary for your travelers. Include:
    *   The guide's name and direct mobile phone number.
    *   The emergency contact number for the local tour company.
    *   A copy of the "Back-to-Ship" and "Missed Port" refund policies.
    *   Instructions on exactly where to meet the guide just outside the port security gate.

***

Please remember that local conditions can change. It is always wise to double-check with the local operator a week before your clients depart to ensure the guide's contact information remains correct and the weather permits a safe journey.
