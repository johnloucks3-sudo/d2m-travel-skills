## 1. EXCURSION READINESS FLAGS

**Day 2 · Aug 30 · Stockholm — Highlights of Stockholm & Vasa Museum · 09:00**
- Standard coach + walking tour. Gamla Stan cobblestones are uneven — clients with knee/ankle issues should wear good walking shoes, not dress shoes.
- Vasa Museum is fully indoor, flat, accessible. No physical flag.
- 09:00 departure is civil — ship breakfast easily accomplished. No logistics stress.
- Weather: 18–21°C, layers recommended. No rain shelter needed inside museums.

**Day 4 · Sep 1 · Warnemünde/Berlin — The Berlin Experience (SG) · 07:30**
- ⚠️ 07:30 is the earliest departure of the voyage. Ship breakfast likely not open or just opening. Flag for group logistics (see Section 4).
- Berlin round trip is 3+ hours transit each way. Full day on feet — museums, monuments, outdoor walking. Moderate fitness assumed; anyone with mobility concerns needs to know this is a long, high-step day.
- Brandenburg Gate / Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe: outdoor, uneven surfaces, no shade. Sep 1 in Berlin: 19–22°C, bring layers, comfortable shoes mandatory.
- "SG" designation likely Small Group — confirm with Regent portal. If confirmed, better pacing than large coach tours.

**Day 5 · Sep 2 · Warnemünde/Berlin — Amazing Rostock · 09:00**
- Recovery day compared to Berlin. Rostock is a walking pace city — cobbled historic market, Marienkirche, outdoor café culture.
- Low physical demand. Good for anyone who pushed hard the day before.
- ⚠️ Clients may feel this is a step down after Berlin. Worth framing in Dani's email as a deliberate contrast — slower, more residential, a different kind of German city.
- 09:00 is comfortable. No early logistics flag.

**Day 6 · Sep 3 · Copenhagen — A Tour of Two Kingdoms · 08:45**
- Kronborg Castle (Elsinore): significant walking on castle grounds, some uneven stone. Not prohibitive but worth noting for clients who struggled in Berlin.
- Frederiksborg Palace: manicured grounds, well-surfaced paths, accessible interior. Lower physical demand than Kronborg.
- Øresund Bridge transit is seated coach. Long excursion — plan for 6–8 hours total.
- Dress: Copenhagen is fashion-forward. Smart casual appropriate; the Two Kingdoms excursion mixes outdoor castle grounds with palace interiors — layers required.
- Returns clients to ship mid/late afternoon, freeing the overnight for Copenhagen evening dining.

**Day 7 · Sep 4 · Copenhagen — Tivoli Gardens & Canal Cruise · 08:45**
- Canal Cruise: seated, flat, easy. Zero physical flag.
- Tivoli: flat gardens, paved paths throughout. Fully accessible. Evening light in early September is excellent — encourage clients to linger past excursion drop-off if schedule allows.
- ⚠️ Excursion likely returns to ship by early afternoon. Clients have rest of day + evening free in Copenhagen. This is the best free evening of the voyage — second Copenhagen dining opportunity.
- Weather Sep 4: same as Sep 3, evenings cooling to 11–13°C. A good jacket or scarf matters for outdoor Tivoli seating.

**Day 9 · Sep 6 · Kristiansand — Explore Kristiansand on Foot · 10:00**
- 10:00 is the latest departure of the voyage. No morning logistics pressure at all.
- Walking tour — Posebyen (cobbled streets), Christiansholm Fortress (ramparts, uneven surfaces), harbor waterfront.
- ⚠️ "On Foot" means exactly that — no coach. Clients with knee or balance issues should be flagged to bring trekking poles or good ankle support. Not strenuous, but sustained walking on historic surfaces.
- Fiskebrygga harbor area has excellent local seafood options post-excursion if clients have free time after.
- Weather: 15–18°C, wind possible near harbor and fortress ramparts. Packable windbreaker recommended.

**Day 10 · Sep 7 · Oslo — Hadeland Glass Works & Fram Museum · 09:30**
- Hadeland Glass Works: largely indoor — glass-blowing workshop, shops, restaurants. Accessible, flat, easy for any fitness level. The grounds are worth a walk (uneven terrain but optional).
- Fram Museum: indoor, flat, excellent for all mobility levels. Significant visual/emotional payoff with minimal physical demand.
- ⚠️ Hadeland is 60km north of Oslo — this is a full coach excursion day. Not a stroll around the ship's port. Clients should know they're committing the day.
- ⚠️ Intel note: Hadeland glamping tent season closes Sep 8. Excursion is Sep 7 — should be fine, but verify with Regent tour desk that grounds activities are still fully operational.
- Returns clients to Oslo for the final evening. Evening dining opportunity on the last night of the voyage.

## 2. DINING RECOMMENDATIONS BY PORT

### Copenhagen (Sep 3–4 overnight)

**Geranium** — currently ranked #1 in Scandinavia, three-Michelin-star, complete New Nordic tasting experience. Requires advance reservation (typically 6–8 weeks out for Sep). If clients haven't booked, this is now likely unavailable — surface as aspirational reference only, not a booking recommendation for TP 1.1.

**Höst** — Recommended. Nørre Farimagsgade 41. Modern Nordic, intimate room, excellent wine program, warm service. Accessible without 3-month lead time; reasonable advance booking (2–3 weeks) typically secures a table for 6 in early September. Price: $$$ (not astronomical). Atmosphere: exactly right for a group of luxury travelers who want a genuine Copenhagen meal without a tasting menu marathon. *Why it fits: group-friendly table sizing, warm Nordic aesthetic, strong on local seasonal ingredients, service level matches Regent clientele expectations.*

**Nyhavn waterfront dining** — Not a single restaurant but worth flagging: post-Tivoli/Canal Cruise (Sep 4 afternoon), the Nyhavn harbor is within easy reach. Café Nyhavn and Cap Horn both accommodate groups without long lead times. More casual than Höst but the setting (colored 17th-century facades, working canal) makes the meal. *Good for the Sep 4 lunch/early dinner slot if clients want atmosphere over gastronomy.*

### Oslo (Sep 7 evening free)

**Maaemo** — Three Michelin stars, Dronning Eufemias gate 23. Oslo's benchmark New Nordic restaurant. Same caveat as Geranium: Sep reservations require significant advance booking. Not actionable at TP 1.1 stage — reference only.

**Statholdergaarden** — Recommended. Rådhusgata 11, in a 17th-century mansion. Norwegian-French haute cuisine, exceptional service, group-appropriate private dining rooms available. Advance booking 2–3 weeks typically sufficient for early September. The setting (restored period interior) matches a luxury farewell dinner perfectly. *Why it fits: final-night caliber, handles groups of 6 without squeezing them at a side table, cuisine is familiar enough for clients who don't want to be challenged on the last evening.*

**Aker Brygge waterfront** — Backup option, no reservation required. Oslo's renovated harbor district has multiple restaurants at varying price points. Not the same prestige, but if Statholdergaarden is full, a waterfront meal on the last evening is still a strong experience. Solsiden (seafood, harbor view) is a solid fallback for groups.
