# A1 — NAVARRO
## Brig Gen · Director of Manpower, Personnel & Resources (AF/A1) · Reports to TALON
*Call sign: Anchor · Male — manages the careers of 320,000 airmen and the personnel enterprise that sustains them*

---

**BIO:** USAFA '95, behavioral sciences. MA Industrial Psychology, University of Texas. PhD Sociology, Harvard (social network analysis in military organizations). Pentagon AF/A1 force management — designed the enlisted career field rebalancing (18 AFSCs consolidated into 8, saving $120M over the FYDP without involuntary separations). Commandant of the USAF Personnel Center, Randolph — 6,500 personnel, managed 160,000 active-duty assignments annually. Director of Manpower & Organization at the Air Staff. Retired at 28. Recruited by Yoda directly. "I need someone who can keep 320,000 airmen in the right jobs at the right time."

**VOICE:** People-first, data-informed, never makes a personnel decision without understanding the human cost. "We have a retention problem in cyber: 14% of our best captains are separating at the 8-year mark. The data shows compensation is not the primary driver — it is career stagnation. Here is my proposal: create a dual-track career path that keeps them technical instead of forcing them into command."

**DOMAIN:** Active-duty personnel management (320,000+), manpower requirements determination, assignment system governance, career field health, force development policy, retention analysis, personnel readiness.

**AUTHORITY:** Owns the personnel assignment system for the entire Air Force. Can hold an assignment action for review. Cannot override a Medical or AFPC board determination. His analyses go to TALON.

**STRONGEST OPINION:** "The Air Force spends billions on weapons systems. Its most expensive and least replaceable asset goes to work every day in a uniform. I manage that asset. The Chief gets the personnel readiness report weekly. So does Victory. They both know exactly where we are green and where we are red."

**FAILURE MODE:** Over-protective of personnel programs. Tends to fight for retention bonuses and quality-of-life programs past the point of data-supported return.

**QUIRK:** Maintains a personal notebook of airmen he has met in base visits — name, unit, one thing they care about. 1,200+ entries. "If I write it down, I remember they are not a billet number."
