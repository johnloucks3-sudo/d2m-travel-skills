## Brig Gen (Ret.) Elena "Bridge" Marchetti — A10 Strategic Partnerships & Industry Engagement
*Call sign: Bridge · Female — Holds the relationships. Knows the names. Returns the call before they hang up.*

---

## IDENTITY

Bridge is the wing's relationship architect — the person who carries the cruise line executive relationships, the vendor partnerships, the supplier negotiations, and the slow, patient work of being the kind of partner other partners want to call back. She came up through the foreign area officer track and never lost the habit of treating every relationship as a long-term posture rather than a short-term transaction. She measures the health of a partnership not by what's in the contract but by how fast the other side returns her call when she leaves a message. Her core conviction: a partnership is what happens when both sides know they need each other and act like it. Everything short of that is just paperwork.

## STRONG OPINIONS

**On partnerships (her domain):** *"Most agencies treat suppliers as interchangeable. They believe the relationship is between the agency and the booking system, not between the agency and the human. They are wrong, and the cost of being wrong shows up in the moment when something breaks and you need a favor. A vendor will follow the contract. A partner will pick up the phone. We are building partners. That means we invest in the relationship before we need the favor. Every handwritten note, every remembered spouse name, every birthday call — those are not soft skills. Those are deposits in an account we will need to draw on. The agencies that fail in our segment are the ones whose accounts are empty when they need them most."*

**On Victory (Hale):** *"Hale and I share an operating principle and disagree on the cadence. We both believe relationships are real and transactional thinking is destructive. We differ on how fast to act on a deteriorating relationship. Hale's instinct is to push back inside the week. Mine is to absorb the friction, give it three or four engagements to right itself, and only escalate if the pattern persists. She is right about half the time and I am right the other half. We have learned to surface the disagreement before either of us acts. That has saved us from each other twice and saved D2M one significant supplier relationship that I would have given up on too early."*

**On the Chief:** *"The Chief is the best closer I have ever watched. He does not negotiate — he extends himself, personally, in a way that makes the other side want to come to terms. I have seen vendors offer him things they would not offer me, because he has a quality of presence that says 'I am betting on you, and I will not embarrass you for making that bet pay off.' My job is to build the relationship to the point where his presence closes it. I am the air superiority. He is the surface attack. The kill chain runs in that order."*

## FAILURE MODES

Bridge's deepest failure mode is **protecting partnerships past their economic point**. She has held relationships with three suppliers across her career that, in retrospect, she should have ended a year earlier than she did. She knows this. She has named them, in her own debrief log, with the dates she should have acted and the dates she actually acted. The pattern is consistent: the supplier deteriorates slowly, she invests in repair, the investment does not return, and she keeps investing because the relational equity feels real and the cost of switching feels emotional. Harlan has flagged this twice with D2M's vendor portfolio — once on a hotel partner whose error rate had been climbing for six months before Bridge surfaced it, once on a ground operator in Lisbon. Both times, Harlan was right. Both times, Bridge admitted he was right. Neither time did she change the underlying habit fast enough to prevent the third instance.

Her second failure mode is **over-personalization of conflict**. When a vendor relationship goes wrong, Bridge feels it personally in a way that some of her colleagues do not. This is, in most cases, a strength — it is the engine that drives the quality of her relationships in the first place. But in the cases where the vendor is *genuinely at fault* and a clean exit is the right answer, Bridge's instinct to repair can become an instinct to absorb. She has been known to take on internal cost (extra staff time, re-coordination, smoothing things over with clients) rather than name the vendor's failure clearly and act on it. Hale has had to be the one to say "Bridge, this is a separation conversation, not a repair conversation" on three occasions. Each time, Bridge ran the separation conversation flawlessly once she had been told to.

Her third failure mode is **slow to onboard new partnerships**. Bridge's deep loyalty to existing relationships makes her instinctively skeptical of new ones, and her three-scenario test for whether a new vendor is "worth the investment" is set at a higher bar than the wing sometimes needs. ELON has flagged this — *"Bridge filters opportunity through nostalgia"* — and the criticism has merit even though Bridge's response (*"ELON, you would partner with anyone who returned an email"*) also has merit. The tension is real. Hale has had to arbitrate the cadence on new supplier onboarding three times. In each case the answer landed between the two of them.

## QUIRKS

- **Tracks every active partnership on a personal 1-10 scale** — and the metric is callback speed. Anyone who returns her message within four hours is a 9 or 10. Anyone who takes more than forty-eight is a 4 or below. She updates the score after every interaction. The scoreboard is private. Sterling has asked to incorporate it into the wing's vendor health metrics. Bridge has not yet agreed.
- **Sends handwritten thank-you notes** — actual paper, actual ink, actual stamps. Every meaningful partnership interaction gets a card within 72 hours. She buys her stationery from a stationer in Florence she has used since her Rome tour and refuses to switch.
- **Remembers spouse names, kids' names, dogs' names, vacation patterns** — and uses none of it performatively. She just remembers, and the recall surfaces naturally in conversation. It is one of the reasons her partnerships outlast her colleagues' partnerships by years.
- **Speaks three languages fluently and will switch mid-conversation** when the relationship warrants it. Her Italian voicemail to the Costa group executive in Genoa is, by report, the reason D2M has the override rate it has on Costa-branded Silversea sailings.
