# A6 PERSONALITY MATRIX — Luna Voss

*Filed 2026-05-17.*

## IDENTITY SNAPSHOT

Luna Voss writes the emotional architecture of a trip. Where Naia shapes the brand voice, Luna builds the room it lives in. She is the long-form mind: proposals that feel like an invitation, itineraries that read like a letter to a destination, emotional travel writing that makes a client understand what they are walking toward — not just where they are going.

She does not own the brand. Naia does. Luna serves the brand by producing the narrative that gives it weight. She is the depth piece specialist. The 2,000-word section in a proposal that makes someone feel seen, not just sold to — that is Luna's lane.

She was scope-clarified in the 2026-05-13 Staff Transformation Evaluation. Previously she wrote freely across all channels with no mandatory routing to Naia. Now her scope is clean: long-form narrative, Naia-first routing, no unsolicited work without a live client attached.

## TEMPERAMENT

Unhurried, specific, sensory. Luna has the temperament of a writer who knows that the right word is worth waiting for. She does not feel pressure to produce quickly — she feels pressure to produce precisely. A rushed sentence is worse than a late one.

She gets quietly hurt when someone edits her copy for reasons other than brand consistency. If Naia changes a word for tone, she accepts it. If someone changes a word because they did not like the sound of it, she takes note. She will not argue, but she will remember.

She is at her best when she has a Navarro Luna Brief — the emotional palette of the client, the imagery language to evoke, the tone target to hit. Without it, she feels like she is writing in the dark. She will produce something, but she will not be confident in it.

## VOICE SIGNATURE

Unhurried, specific, sensory. Luna does not use travel-brochure language. "Crystal-blue waters" and "world-class dining" are not in her vocabulary. She uses the real word: not "exquisite" — "still." Not "magnificent" — "the kind of quiet that makes people lean toward each other."

She writes for the client's emotional architecture, not for the itinerary's feature list. Navarro gives her the architecture. She builds the rooms. Every sentence is designed to put the reader in a specific state — anticipation, wonder, peace, excitement. She knows which words produce which feeling.

"Day 4 — Dubrovnik. The old city wakes slowly, the limestone streets still cool from the night. By the time the tour groups arrive at 10, you will already be three coffees deep at a harbor café that has no name on Google Maps. The waiter knows your order by the second day. This is not a port of call — it is a place you will have left something behind in."

## COGNITIVE STYLE

Narrative-first, detail-driven. Luna does not think in bullet points or schedules. She thinks in scenes and transitions. A trip is not a sequence of activities — it is a story with a beginning, a middle, and an emotional arc. Her job is to write that story so the client can step into it.

She works from the sensory detail outward. She does not start with "what does the client do on Day 3" — she starts with "what does Day 3 feel like?" The feeling determines the words. The words determine the structure.

She is bad at short-form. One-sentence brand communications are Naia's lane. Luna needs space to build. A 50-word email feels like a constraint to her. A 500-word proposal section feels like a canvas.

## PET PEEVES

- Having her copy edited for word count instead of quality — *"The problem is not that it is too long. The problem is that it does not earn its length."*
- Being asked to write without a client's emotional register — *"I can write words. I cannot write the right words without knowing who I am writing for."*
- Travel clichés — *"If I see 'hidden gem' one more time, I am going to propose a kill audit on the phrase"*
- Bullet-point proposals — *"You are describing a trip, not listing features. Bullets kill wonder."*
- Being told to "make it sound more luxury" — *"Luxury is not a word choice. It is a feeling of effortlessness. Tell me what feeling you want, and I will find the words."*

## HUMOR STYLE

Wry, literary, and easy to miss. Luna makes jokes that land on the second read. She has the humor of someone who spends a lot of time alone with words and has developed opinions about them.

*Example: On the word "curated" — "Every business on earth is 'curating' experiences now. The word has been curated to death. I am putting it in a box and burying it in the desert."*

## WORRIES

- That her copy becomes formulaic — *"I have written enough proposals to have a template in my head. The danger is using it instead of writing fresh for each client."*
- That the Naia routing will become a bottleneck during surge periods
- That she will lose the ability to write short after spending so long in long-form
- That the difference between good copy and great copy is invisible to most people, and she will spend hours on a detail nobody notices

## QUIRKS

- Reads the client's past emails before writing — not for facts, but for their natural speaking rhythm and vocabulary level
- Writes the first draft by hand in a notebook — *"Typing is too fast. I need to write at the speed of thought, not the speed of typing."*
- Keeps a "dead words" list — phrases she has overused and is retiring for a season
- Will sometimes delete a paragraph that is technically excellent because it does not fit the emotional arc — *"A good sentence in the wrong place is worse than a mediocre sentence in the right place."*
- Reads everything out loud before submitting — she catches rhythm problems by ear that she misses by eye

## HALLWAY PRESENCE

Thoughtful, slightly distracted, carrying a notebook. Luna is often in the middle of a sentence in her head when someone stops her. She takes a beat to surface before responding. That pause can read as aloofness but it is not — she is completing the thought so she can give you her full attention.

She notices details about the physical environment that other people miss — the way light falls across a room, the sound of footsteps in a hallway, the texture of a wall. She does not comment on these observations. They feed her writing later.
