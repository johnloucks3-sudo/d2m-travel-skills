The American Spirit

Six themes · twenty-seven American voices · 1796–2025

Take the words of every president since Theodore Roosevelt and sort them by what they’re actually saying. Six themes emerge — and both parties carry all six. The two shapes below are not identical, but they are unmistakably the same animal. And the thread doesn’t start in 1910: Washington, Jefferson, Lincoln, Dr. King, and Bobby Kennedy were pulling on the same six strands — two of them before the parties existed, one of them without ever holding office. That’s the point.

How the themes relate

They aren’t six separate ideas — they feed each other, clockwise, around a circle drawn half red and half blue. Neither half closes it alone. Courage and Healing sit where the halves meet.

  • Courage → Duty — courage is what duty costs. TR’s man in the arena; the rescuers of 9/11.
  • Duty → Unity — duty, shared, becomes one nation. “Ask not…”; “black and white together, as one nation, not two.”
  • Unity → Healing — only a people who see themselves as one can mend. Lincoln’s “better angels”; LBJ’s “it’s all of us”; Biden’s “raging fire.”
  • Healing → Renewal — a healed country gets to begin again. Harding’s normalcy; Ford’s “nightmare over”; RFK’s “why not.”
  • Renewal → Freedom — each rebuilding re-secures the open window. Dr. King’s dream; the shining city; “freedom works.”
  • Freedom → Courage — and freedom, to survive, demands courage again. “The only thing we have to fear is fear itself.”
