# Silver Nova vs. Regent Grandeur: What the Brochure Won't Tell You

The sales pitch for both ships is nearly identical: all-inclusive, ultra-luxury, every cabin a suite, award-winning dining, extraordinary destinations. If you read both websites without already knowing the difference, you'll come away having absorbed the word "extraordinary" eleven times and knowing almost nothing useful.

I've sailed on both. Here's what I actually noticed.

---

## The Ships

**Silver Nova** — Silversea's newest ship, launched 2023. Carries 728 guests. 365 suites. Built in Italy (Fincantieri). The S.A.L.T. (Sea and Land Taste) culinary program is her most talked-about feature, designed around immersive regional food experiences tied to each itinerary.

**Seven Seas Grandeur** — Regent's flagship, launched 2023. Carries 746 guests. 373 suites. Also Italian-built (Fincantieri). She replaced the Seven Seas Mariner as the newest and largest ship in the Regent fleet, and came with significant design investment — notably the new Pacific Rim dining venue and an expanded spa deck.

They're almost exactly the same size. Built the same year. Built in the same shipyard. And they are meaningfully different ships.

---

## The Food

This is where the divergence starts.

**Silversea / Silver Nova:** The S.A.L.T. program is real. I was skeptical before the Pacific crossing — a corporate culinary initiative designed around "local ingredients" sounded like hotel branding dressed as restaurant philosophy. It isn't. The S.A.L.T. Kitchen venue shifts its menu based on the itinerary in a way that's genuinely tied to the region. On the Nova, during the Japan segment, the kitchen was working with ingredients that I didn't recognize from American dining and that the culinary team could explain with specificity. The S.A.L.T. Lab (cooking class format) is worth booking early.

The main dining room, La Terrazza, holds up well. The specialty venues — S.A.L.T. Kitchen, Silver Note (jazz supper club), Atlantide — each have a distinct identity.

**Regent / Grandeur:** Regent's culinary reputation is built on Compass Rose, the main dining restaurant, and it earns that reputation. Compass Rose is the best main dining room I've sat in at sea — the room is large but manages to feel proportional, the service is polished without being stiff, and the menu offers enough variety that it sustains daily visits. The Pacific Rim venue is strong. Chartreuse (French-inspired) is excellent for a longer dinner.

The distinction: Silversea's food surprises you. Regent's food satisfies you. That's not a criticism of either — it's a useful distinction for knowing which experience you want.

---

## The All-Inclusive

Both are all-inclusive. The execution differs.

**Silversea:** All-inclusive means flights are included on longer voyages (varies by fare), shore excursions are available but many are at cost, alcohol/minibar/gratuities included. Silversea's butler service in suite categories above Superior Veranda is genuine — the butlers are trained, proactive, and remember preferences.

**Regent:** All-inclusive means shore excursions included (this is significant), flights included on most itineraries, alcohol/minibar/gratuities included. Regent's included excursions are the headline differentiator in this comparison — on a two-week itinerary, free excursions save $300–800+ per person depending on destinations. The quality of included excursions ranges from excellent to routine; the better ones book out early.

If shore excursions matter to your budget calculation, Regent wins on paper. If the excursions included don't interest you (or if you prefer independent exploration), that advantage disappears.

---

## The Cabin

Both lines offer all-suite ships. The suite categories differ in what you get at entry level.

**Silversea Superior Veranda Suite:** Approximately 312 sq ft plus veranda. Well-designed, generous storage, sitting area, full marble bathroom. The veranda is proper — width and depth sufficient for two chairs and a small table. This is where I'd start for a first Silversea voyage.

**Regent Deluxe Veranda Suite:** Approximately 307 sq ft plus veranda. Similar configuration. The shower is notably good — larger than you'd expect on a ship, real pressure. The entertainment system is newer. The cabin finishes on Grandeur feel slightly more contemporary.

At entry level, these cabins are close enough to call it a draw. The difference opens up at higher suite categories — Regent's penthouse and Master Suite categories are among the most impressive cabin spaces at sea. Silversea's Owner's Suite is comparable.

---

## The Guests

I'm going to say what travel agents usually don't say.

**Silversea guests** tend to be curious travelers. This is a line built around expedition itineraries and culinary depth, and it attracts people who are interested in the world in a particular way. There's more variety in demographic profile than the marketing suggests — yes, it skews older and affluent, but the S.A.L.T. program and the expedition DNA bring in a subset of guests who are genuinely enthusiastic about learning. The conversation at dinner is often interesting.

**Regent guests** tend to be experienced cruisers. This is a line for people who know exactly what they want and have booked enough cruises to recognize when they're receiving it. The standard is high because the guests hold it high. The energy onboard is confident and relaxed in equal measure.

Neither description is better. But one may match your travel personality better than the other.

---

## The Bottom Line

The decision between Silver Nova and Regent Grandeur usually comes down to two questions:

**1. Do included shore excursions matter to you?**
If yes: Regent. On a two-week itinerary, the value is real. If the included excursions match the destinations you want to explore, Regent's all-inclusive is genuinely more all-inclusive.

**2. Do you want to be surprised by what you eat?**
If yes: Silversea. The S.A.L.T. program delivers an experience that's distinct from every other ship I've been on. If food is an important part of how you travel, the Silver Nova is worth serious consideration.

My honest recommendation: I've put clients on both ships and gotten enthusiastic reports back from both. These are two of the finest ships currently sailing. The choice between them is a preference question, not a quality question.
