# Boarding Silver Nova in Yokohama: What We Noticed in the First Hour

*By John A. Loucks III, CEO, Dreams2Memories Travel, LLC*

---

We boarded Silver Nova at 11:45 in the morning at Yokohama. The embarkation process was smooth — efficient check-in, no long lines, no confusion about where to go. If you've done a major embarkation port during peak season, you know that smooth is not guaranteed, and Yokohama delivered.

What happened next is worth talking about, because it reveals something true about the difference between Silversea and Regent Seven Seas — two lines I get asked to compare constantly.

---

## What Was Missing

On Regent, the moment you step aboard someone is waiting with a champagne glass. It's not just a nice gesture; it's a signal. It says: you've arrived, the experience has started, you matter. That ritual, repeated across every Regent boarding I've done, sets a tone that carries through the entire voyage.

On Silver Nova, nobody was waiting with champagne.

I want to be precise about this: the crew we encountered were gracious. The ship was immaculate. The embarkation itself, as I said, was easy. What was absent was that specific moment of greeting — the immediate, physical acknowledgment that you're a guest and the experience has begun.

It's a small thing and it's a significant thing. If you're coming to Silver Nova from a Regent sailing, notice it and adjust your expectations in the first hour. The ship delivers at a very high level — but it doesn't deliver in the same way, and the opening minute is where that difference is most visible.

To compound the small frustration: the restaurants weren't open for another fifteen minutes after we boarded. When you've flown to Yokohama, gotten through embarkation, and you're ready to sit down and exhale, fifteen minutes in a holding pattern feels longer than it is.

---

## Where to Actually Go in the First Hour

Here is the practical intelligence I wish I'd had when we boarded.

We went to Dolce Vita, the bar on Deck 5. It's a pleasant space — relaxed, good sightlines, and the natural first stop when you're aboard and nothing else is open yet. The staff there were attentive and the drinks were fine.

But if you want food — which we did — **go to Arts Cafe on Deck 4 instead.** 

Arts Cafe was open. We didn't know this until later in the voyage, and it's the kind of thing that doesn't appear in the brochure. If you're boarding Silver Nova, especially at a mid-morning embarkation like ours, the Arts Cafe is where you want to be: casual, relaxed, food available, no wait for a table. Pass this on to anyone sailing Silver Nova.

---

## The Suite: Premium Medallion, Stern

We were in a Premium Medallion Suite, and I want to say something specific about the balcony.

Rear-facing suites on Silver Nova have a 270-degree view from the stern. Not a side-facing balcony with ocean to your right or left — a wrap-around view of the wake behind the ship, the horizon expanding in three directions. When the ship is at sea and you're standing on that balcony, you're watching where you've been disappear into the Pacific. 

It's a different experience from a forward-facing or midship suite. Some people prefer the sense of forward motion and what's ahead. I found myself spending more time on that balcony than I expected, and the view was part of it. If you're choosing between suite categories on Silver Nova and a stern cabin is available, it's worth knowing what you're getting.

The suite itself was well-appointed — well-designed storage, a proper bathroom, good natural light. Nothing in the cabin struck us as a problem.

---

## The Honest First-Hour Summary

What Silversea does right: the ship is beautiful, the experience is genuine, and once you're settled it delivers at a level that justifies the fare. The Arts Cafe intelligence alone, if you use it, saves you a frustrating first half-hour.

What Silversea does differently from Regent: the boarding moment is quieter. If the champagne greeting and the immediate sense of ceremony matter to you — and for some clients they genuinely do — Regent's first hour is warmer. Silversea's product runs deeper in other ways, and by day two the comparison had largely faded. But I'm being honest about the first hour because that's the hour I'm most frequently asked about.

The next nineteen days earned back every bit of that first-hour gap and more. But the gap was there, and you should know it going in.
