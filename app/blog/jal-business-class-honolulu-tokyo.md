# JAL Business Class, Honolulu to Tokyo: Honest Notes from Seats 6G and 6D

*By John A. Loucks III, CEO, Dreams2Memories Travel, LLC*

---

Nine hours over the Pacific. My wife Susie in 6D, me in 6G, JAL Flight 73 from Honolulu to Haneda. We had just spent five nights at the Hale Koa in Waikiki and we were headed to Tokyo before boarding *Silver Nova* in Yokohama. This was the middle leg of a 32-day journey, and it turned out to be one of the more memorable flights I've taken.

Here is what I can tell you about JAL Business Class on that route, honestly.

---

## The Seat

Ergonomic is the right word. JAL's Sky Suite configuration is well-engineered — the engineers clearly thought about the geometry of a human body over nine hours, not just the marketing pitch of "lie-flat." The recline is smooth, the position options are genuinely useful, and nothing jabs you in an inconvenient place after hour four.

Privacy: better than most of what American carriers are offering right now. If you've flown United Polaris, American Flagship Business, or Delta One on a long-haul route, you'll notice the improvement. The pods are more enclosed, the sight lines to neighboring passengers are better managed. That said, it's not Turkish Airlines. If you've flown Turkish Business Class across the Atlantic — the herringbone configuration, the generous separation — JAL doesn't quite match that level of privacy. It's a real comparison and it's worth being honest about.

Seats 6G and 6D are paired. We were sitting next to each other with two center consoles between us — you can see each other, you can talk, and with a little effort you can share a tray. It's not the side-by-side sofa configuration of some products, but for a couple it works. We weren't marooned in separate pods staring at opposite walls for nine hours.

I slept. Not deeply, not effortlessly, but I slept. The seat goes flat enough for a reasonable rest on an overnight Pacific crossing. I wouldn't call it the transcendent sleep experience of a European first class product, but this is business class and nine hours is manageable. I arrived in Tokyo tired but functional, which is the correct standard.

---

## The Food and Drink

This is where JAL earns its reputation and then some.

The bento box is the answer. I've eaten hundreds of airline meals across four decades of flying and I cannot think of another carrier that gets it this right. JAL serves a proper bento — compartmented, composed, thoughtfully assembled — and the quality of what's inside is a category above what passes for business class food on US carriers. It is genuinely what you'd eat at a good restaurant in Japan. That's not a small thing at 35,000 feet.

The sake. Two types, both excellent. This is the specific detail I tell every client who asks about the flight: the sake program on JAL is serious. They're not pouring something perfunctory because you asked for a Japanese drink. The steward who served us that evening — attentive, personable, clearly proud of what he was offering — walked us through both options and had genuine enthusiasm about it. That level of service, unprompted and warm, is what you remember.

---

## Landing at Haneda

Haneda instead of Narita is not a small difference and it's worth understanding before you book.

Narita is an international hub 60 to 75 minutes from central Tokyo by train or car, depending on traffic. Haneda is 30 minutes from the city center, newer inside, and significantly more pleasant to arrive in after a long flight. The airport itself is well-designed — it doesn't have that particular combination of exhaustion and distance that Narita inflicts on international arrivals.

I will say this: getting a taxi or Uber out of Haneda is still genuinely confusing. Japan's ride-hailing situation for foreign visitors remains complicated — app-based services don't work the way you expect, the taxi queue system requires navigation if you don't speak Japanese, and signage for international arrivals isn't as clear as the airport's otherwise excellent design would suggest. Budget extra time and patience for the ground exit regardless of how easy the airport looks.

---

## Would I Book JAL Again?

Yes, if the value equation works. That's the honest answer.

When I'm advising clients on the HNL–NRT or HNL–HND route, JAL is competitive if the fares are in the same range as the US carriers. The food and sake program, the personable service, the ergonomic seat, and the Haneda advantage add up to a meaningfully better experience than what United, American, or Delta are selling on that route at equivalent price points.

If JAL is significantly more expensive — and sometimes it is — the premium needs to be weighed against what you're getting. For us, it was worth it. For most clients making a Japan trip of two weeks or more, getting the journey started the right way matters. The flight sets the tone.

Nine hours, sake, bento, a functional night's sleep, and we landed at Haneda at 5:30 in the morning ready for Tokyo. I'll take that every time.
