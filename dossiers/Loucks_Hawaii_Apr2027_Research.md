## PART 2 — 2028 MULTI-GEN ISLAND SURVEY

### What Changes for Multi-Gen

Multi-generation Hawaii (Loucks family + extended family, likely with grandchildren) has different success criteria than a couples trip:

- **Space:** Villas or multi-room suites vs. hotel rooms
- **Kids programs:** Structured, quality programming keeps parents sane and kids engaged
- **Multiple pool options:** Kids and adults each need their own speed
- **Accessibility:** Flat layouts, lagoon/protected water (not open surf beach) important for mixed-mobility group
- **Ease of logistics:** On-property dining, activities, transport — fewer decisions the better
- **Island choice:** Not all islands are equally accessible (direct flights) or equally multi-gen friendly

### Island Comparison — Multi-Gen Lens

| Island | Pros for Multi-Gen | Considerations |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Maui (Wailea)** | Best resort concentration, calmest south-facing beaches, excellent kids programs at major properties, best flight access (direct from DEN on United/Southwest seasonal) | More expensive than Oahu; resort corridor isolated from authentic Hawaii |
| **Big Island (Kohala-Hualalai)** | Most dramatic landscape, Four Seasons Hualalai (the best multi-gen property in Hawaii by most luxury benchmarks), snorkel at Kahaluu, stargazing | Longer internal drives; two airports; remoteness adds logistics |
| **Kauai (Poipu/Princeville)** | Most natural/scenic, Grand Hyatt excellent value, Grand Hyatt lazy river beloved by families | Least commercial = fewer options; Princeville is North Shore (different crowd) |
| **Oahu (Ko Olina)** | Four Seasons Ko Olina has strong family programming, protected lagoons (safe for all ages/abilities), near Pearl Harbor/PCC for historic day trips | Most urban of the islands; Ko Olina is isolated from Waikiki |

### Top 5 Multi-Gen Properties (2028 Planning)

#### MG-1 — Four Seasons Resort Hualalai (Big Island, Kohala Coast) — GOLD STANDARD
- Why it tops the list: Consistently ranked #1 or #2 luxury resort in Hawaii overall. The only property that matches Silversea/Regent service on land across the entire state. Custom family itineraries, private snorkeling ponds with manta rays, cultural programs, multiple pools. Four Seasons Kids for All Seasons program (ages 5–12).
- Villa/space: Bungalow-style rooms clustered by pool — no high-rise feel. Multi-unit bungalows available.
- Rate: est. ~$1,500–$3,500/night (suites/villas range widely). Multi-gen groups book multiple connecting bungalows.
- Mobility: Single-story, flat layout, golf cart service throughout. Excellent.
- 2028 consideration: Book 18–24 months in advance for peak dates — most sought-after property in Hawaii for luxury travelers. Would need to be initiated ~mid-2026 to ensure prime inventory.
- Best for: John and Susie as the anchor couple + adult children/spouses + grandchildren. True five-star experience every generation can participate in.

#### MG-2 — Four Seasons Resort Maui at Wailea — MULTI-GEN PROVEN
- Why it's here: The "family-friendly four seasons" counterpart to Hualalai. Kids for All Seasons program (5–12). Three pools. Beach cabanas. Easy Wailea flat-walk restaurant row. Very strong service.
- Villa/space: Suites and connecting rooms available. No villa-style multi-bedroom like Hualalai; larger parties need multiple rooms.
- Rate: est. ~$900–$2,500/night. Multi-gen group would need 4–6 rooms = significant budget.
- Mobility: Flat, beach-level layout — excellent for John's preference.
- 2028 note: Wailea is closest to Kapalua, Lahaina (rebuilding), and Hana day-trip. Maui has the most multi-gen itinerary depth of any island.
- Best for: Groups where the parents + older grandchildren want a broader activity menu.

#### MG-3 — Grand Wailea Resort, Maui (Waldorf Astoria) — FAMILIES WITH YOUNG CHILDREN
- Why it's here: Nine pools, four waterslides, lazy river, rope swing, cave grotto — purpose-built for families with young grandchildren. "Grand Passport" activity map for kids. Partnered with local childcare.
- Note: Service tier is notably below Four Seasons Maui despite being nearby and same price tier. More resort-camp feel than luxury.
- Rate: est. ~$700–$1,500/night. Better value than Four Seasons for comparable scale.
- Best for: If grandchildren are young (under 10) and the waterpark experience drives the decision. Less compelling if adults want a calm, high-service environment.

#### MG-4 — Four Seasons Ko Olina, Oahu — OAHU MULTI-GEN ANCHOR
- Why it's here: Same property reviewed in Part 1, but in a multi-gen context it shines differently. Four protected lagoons are the safest, calmest swimming environment in Hawaii — ideal for all ages and mixed-mobility groups. Strong kids programs. Proximity to Pearl Harbor (30 min) is meaningful for a USAFA commander's multi-gen trip — Pearl Harbor + USS Missouri + Pacific Aviation Museum is a day that lands across all generations.
- Rate: est. ~$1,100–$1,800/night per room.
- Mobility: Best flat, protected layout of any Hawaii luxury resort. Top marks.
- 2028 advantage: Oahu has the most flight access options from Colorado (direct DEN-HNL on multiple carriers), making coordination easier for adult children flying from different cities.
- Best for: Groups where accessibility/safety (protected water, flat terrain) is a priority and where historic Oahu day-trips are part of the program.

#### MG-5 — Grand Hyatt Kauai Resort & Spa (Poipu) — VALUE ANCHOR FOR LARGER GROUPS
- Why it's here: Best luxury family value in Hawaii. Strong Kauai natural environment (Napali Coast, Waimea Canyon day-trips). Lazy river, multiple pools, Camp Hyatt (ages 3–12). Koloa Landing next door (Marriott, villa-style, 25 acres) for larger parties needing multi-bedroom units.
- Rate: est. ~$400–$900/night at Grand Hyatt. Koloa Landing villas are 2BR–3BR at ~$700–$1,400/night — better value per person for a large group.
- Mobility: Flat resort layout, accessible beach.
- Trade-off: Service tier is three-star below Four Seasons/Waldorf. Kauai also has limited dining outside the resort and fewer activity options for large mixed-age groups.
- Best for: Budget-conscious multi-gen trip where natural beauty > service finesse, or as the "second Kauai leg" of a two-island itinerary.
